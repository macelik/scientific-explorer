Bottom line

The workflow is reproducible and its boundaries are stable, but depth alone cannot tell one-copy changes from regional accessibility at this scale, the current segmentation misses moderate focal events, and QC removes clonal homozygous deletions. Phase-oriented allele evidence, modelled per modality and combined with depth, identifies major/minor haplotypes and allele-specific states credibly where both modalities agree. The next change belongs in evidence integration, QC bookkeeping and a depth baseline, leading to an allele-first haplotype-aware caller.

Key findings

  1. The saved X is reproducible from layers/raw with the pinned pyEpi source: 3,449/3,460 cells bit-identical (exp 01). The GC step has no guard for near-zero LOESS predictions (single fragments amplified up to 717×).
  2. QC removes the event it should detect: the bins of the published MCF10-A homozygous CDKN2A/B deletion fail the ≥15%-nonzero-cells bin filter (2 of 3 grid bins removed; exp 02).
  3. Depth contrasts at 2.5 Mb scale are as large as one-copy changes without being events: uncalled random windows span 0.44–1.72× their flanks (5th–95th percentile); the realistic baseline spread is σ_A ≈ 0.39 on the log scale, while a one-copy loss is log 0.5 = −0.69 (exp 04, exp 17).
  4. The current segmentation misses moderate focal events: injected clonal one-copy losses of 20/30/50 bins were detected in 0%/0%/17% of loci and 1.5× gains in 0%/0%/0%, deep (0.1×) losses in 50%/60%/80% (248 semi-synthetic realizations; exp 14). A two-cut scan rescues deep losses but not one-copy changes, because natural windows in the same segment are as extreme (exp 19).
  5. Each modality has an adequate observation model: depth NB2 with exposure (Poisson worse by 0.39 nats per cell-region; no zero inflation needed, exp 09); allele total NB2 conditioned on depth (median α 0.12 vs 0.020 for depth); haplotype split beta-binomial with region-level ρ (exp 15).
  6. Major/minor haplotypes can be identified where there is imbalance: GT phase switches about every 0.5 Mb and cancels the signal when summed; an orientation HMM recovers it (5q13-qter |h−0.5| 0.010 → 0.100, MYC 0.032 → 0.087, chr1q h ≈ 0.71); random-window null 95th percentile 0.035; 31/164 eligible segments imbalanced (exp 16).
  7. Depth + allele together behave sensibly on controls: MYC, 5q13-qter and chr8:115–145 Mb are near-clonal 2:1 gains supported by both modalities (π ≥ 0.95); the deep chr7 dips are flagged 'no modelled state fits' at region level (exp 17, exp 18).
  8. Depth-only calls are not calibrated: the cell-level mixture flags 7/30 uncalled random windows (23%); none of those is supported by both modalities (0/30).

Scope and evidence levels

Development data are MCF10-A scATAC (3,460 cells × 25,337 retained 100 kb bins); nothing here is copy-number truth. [L] local observation from a saved, reproducible experiment; [P] published evidence (MCF10-A karyotype: CDKN2A/B homozygous deletion within a ~4 Mb 9p21 loss, MYC amplification, 19q13-qter and 5q13-qter gains, used only as prespecified weak controls; access limits in sources.json); [I] proposed idea. Every experiment was run by the coordinator or by a delegated agent and validated by the coordinator (independent recomputation or byte-identical rerun). Seeds, environments and commands are in each experiment's summary.json.

Noteworthy findings by pipeline stage

Inputs, QC and preprocessing

  • [L] X = pinned formula on layers/raw (bit-identical 3,449/3,460 cells); 17 cell-bins at near-zero LOESS predictions are 0 historically and unexplained in five environments.
  • [L] The bin filter removed 1,103 autosomal bins incl. CDKN2A/B: clonal homozygous deletions remove themselves.
  • [L] GC correction attenuates low-GC dips (chr7 8.3–10.7: 0.20× raw vs 0.36× in X) and erases the raw 19q13-qter excess (1.82× → 0.93×) whose alleles show imbalance.
  • [L] Rounding zeros are coverage-dependent (median 1.4% vs 0.5% of raw-positive bins, low vs high tercile) but the regional raw contrasts are coverage-invariant.

Segmentation

  • [L] Boundaries reproducible; tests measure reproducible structure, not CN.
  • [L] Moderate focal events are missed (clonal one-copy loss 20–50 bins: 17% at best; 1.5× gains: 0% at best); deep losses are found from ~20 bins.
  • [L] Masking explains part of it (two-cut rescues deep losses); background structure explains the rest (one-copy changes not rescued).

CN assignment and reference

  • [L] pyEpi's scale search always ends at the grid edge (s = 0.5 × median): calls are fixed thresholds on segment mean / genome median (loss < 0.68×, Watson ≤ 0.5×; gain > 1.22×, Watson ≥ 1.5×).
  • [L] Changing the estimand changes 20–37% of bin calls; changing the baseline reverses about one third of called short segments; removing one chromosome changes up to 5% of calls elsewhere.

Depth evidence

  • [L] NB2 with exposure is adequate; zero inflation is not needed.
  • [L] Against uncalled random windows, 27/46 called short losses and 4/21 gains remain unusual.
  • [L] The chr7 saved windows are 0.13–0.34× their flanks, coverage-invariant, shared by all clusters, and ≤ 2.3% of cells could carry a near-complete loss: not explained by a one-copy loss alone.

Allelic evidence and haplotypes

  • [L] Per-modality models: AT NB2 | depth, split beta-binomial with region-level ρ.
  • [L] GT phase switches about every 0.5 Mb; orientation inference is required to see imbalance; it recovers the published gains (5q, MYC) and chr1q (h ≈ 0.71).
  • [L] Nominal AT overstates power; allelic thresholds must be empirical.
  • [L] LOH ascertainment: the 9p21 hemizygous zone has 0.20× the selected-site observations per fragment, and its remaining sites look balanced.

Combined depth + allele

  • [L] Controls with both modalities (MYC, 5q13-qter, chr8:115–145 Mb) come out as near-clonal 2:1 gains at region and cell level.
  • [L] The chr7 dips fit no modelled allele-specific state.
  • [L] Depth-only cell-level calls have a 23% false-positive rate on random windows.

What worked, what is promising, what did not

  • Worked: X reconstruction; locating QC-removed bins; raw-count NB2 models with exposure; held-out model choice per modality; empirical nulls from random windows; phase-orientation HMM; allele-specific state scoring with 'no state fits'; both-modality cell-level calls; the spike-in harness (unmodified scans reproduce saved results exactly).
  • Promising, needs calibration: cell-level mixtures (credible only with both modalities), two-cut scan for strong focal events, distance-aware joint HMM with a baseline-variance term.
  • Did not work / hypotheses rejected: 'the AD p-values are grossly anti-conservative' (rejected, exp 05); 'zero inflation explains the dips' (rejected, exp 09); 'a better search (two-cut) recovers one-copy focal events' (rejected, exp 19); 'nominal AT gives calibrated allelic power' (rejected, exp 06/10/15); sampling-only dispersion for count segmentation (over-segments, exp 11/13); depth-only cell mixtures (23% FPR, exp 18).

Methods explained

Observation models per modality (exp 08, 09, 15)

Depth (E:1, exp 08/09/15). For region r and cell i, raw target counts y with offset log E_i (cell total outside the region) + log(bins), WNN cluster terms; NB2 variance μ + αμ². Chosen because it wins the held-out log score (Poisson worse by 0.39 nats per cell-region; ZINB/hurdle gain ≤ 0.005) and reproduces held-out zero cells; quasi-Poisson gives the same estimates with robust intervals but no likelihood, so NB2 is the one usable in a joint model. Statistical significance against rate ratio 1 is uninformative with thousands of cells; the reference must be the spread of same-width uncalled regions.

Allele total AT (exp 15). NB2 with its own α (median 0.12, ≈ 6× depth's), offset log(raw depth in region + 0.5): AT is not independent of depth (median elasticity 0.50), so it is modelled conditionally. ZINB adds 0.0004 nats.

Haplotype split HB | AT (exp 15). Beta-binomial; binomial is worse by 0.030 nats per cell, a two-component mixture adds nothing; cell-level ρ falls with AT and must be estimated per region (the bin-level 0.0038 is a pooled-bin value).

Why model per modality. The user asked whether modalities need different models; the data say yes: AT is about six times more overdispersed than depth, and the split needs a bounded (beta-binomial) rather than a count model. Using one family for both would mis-weight the allele evidence.

Major/minor haplotype identification (exp 16)

What problem it solves. Allele counts per 100 kb bin (HA, HB) come from SNPs phased by Eagle (GT field, no phase-set IDs). Within a bin the phase is mostly consistent, but between bins the assignment of "haplotype A" and "haplotype B" switches every few bins (statistical phasing switch errors and gaps). In a region where one parental haplotype is truly in excess, GT-oriented per-bin fractions HB/AT are therefore bimodal and mirror-symmetric around 0.5: about half the bins near h and half near 1−h. Summing HB over the region cancels the two modes, which is why the naive estimate was 0.010 at 5q13-qter. The figure below shows the chr1q case: the grey histogram has two peaks, the oriented one a single peak.

In the experiment-16 tracks the HMM does indeed flip bins. It flips whole runs of bins where the GT label put the major haplotype on HA, so every bin reports the fraction of the same (major) haplotype. The flip is justified only because the GT values are bimodal with modes far from 0.5 relative to sampling noise, and mirror-symmetric; where a region is balanced the same procedure gives |h − 0.5| ≤ 0.035 (random-window null), which bounds the folding bias.

Model. For one segment with bins j = 1..n (only bins with allele data), a hidden orientation o_j ∈ {B-major, A-major} follows a two-state Markov chain along the chromosome; the observation is HB_j out of AT_j:

P(o_j ≠ o_(j-1)) = 1 − exp(−λ · d_j),   d_j = bp distance to the previous bin with data, λ = 1 per 5 Mb (prespecified; sensitivity ×0.1, ×10)
HB_j | AT_j, o_j = B-major ~ BetaBinomial(AT_j, p = 0.5 + δ, ρ)
HB_j | AT_j, o_j = A-major ~ BetaBinomial(AT_j, p = 0.5 − δ, ρ)
segment parameter δ ∈ [0, 0.5): profile likelihood over a grid (forward algorithm sums over all orientation paths)
outputs: δ̂, ĥ = 0.5 + δ̂; per-bin posterior P(B-major) (forward–backward); Viterbi switch count; cell-bootstrap CI (200 resamples of cells)
major haplotype per bin = argmax posterior; major counts per cell = HB on B-major bins + HA on A-major bins

Assumptions (each can fail): (1) the imbalance δ is constant within a segment (a segment mixing balanced and imbalanced parts, like 5q 70–118 Mb plus 118–180 Mb, gives an average); (2) the phase within a bin is consistent, so a bin can be oriented as a whole; (3) bins are conditionally independent given the orientation (repeated site counting and shared fragments violate this mildly); (4) ρ = 0.0038 from base-called bins describes pooled-bin overdispersion (experiment 15 showed cell-level ρ is larger, so this model is for pooled bins only); (5) switch hazard λ is the same everywhere; (6) the "major" label is a statement about count asymmetry, not verified parental identity; allele-specific accessibility, reference mapping bias or copy-neutral LOH also create asymmetry.

Why this method. Experiment 06 showed that the phase-agnostic estimator (|HB−HA| per bin) recovers the imbalance but is biased upward at balance (folding) and cannot say which haplotype is major. GT-oriented summing is unbiased at balance but cancels real imbalance. The orientation HMM is the standard way to reconcile the two (it is the mechanism Numbat uses for phase switches); it makes the folding explicit, estimates where the orientation changes, and is calibrated by running it on uncalled random windows. Result: 5q13-qter 0.010 → 0.100, MYC 0.032 → 0.087; 19q13-qter (0.013) and the 9p21 zone (0.015) are not imbalanced by this model. Cross-cluster: large imbalanced segments have the same major haplotype in all clusters with data; cluster 4 on chr8:115–145 Mb has the opposite one (a mirrored-event hypothesis, 182 cells).

Allele-specific state scoring (exp 17)

What it does. For a region it combines two summaries, each with its own likelihood and its own empirical null, and asks which explicit allele-specific state explains both.

depth   L_obs = raw-count rate ratio target / flanks (or / genome for broad loci)      log L_obs ~ Normal(log L_exp, σ_A²)
        σ_A = robust SD of log L among uncalled random windows (≈ 0.39): regional baseline variability, which dominates sampling noise
allele  ĥ = phase-agnostic major-haplotype fraction (exp 10) with SE                   ĥ ~ Normal(max(0.5 + k (h_exp − 0.5), h_null), SE² + τ²)
        τ, h_null from uncalled random windows; k = allelic attenuation (not identified; 1.0 / 0.6 / 0.4)
cells   homozygous-loss states only: zero-count cells among cells expected ≥ 5 fragments ~ Binomial
states  1:1 (none), 1:0, 0:0, 2:0, 2:1, 2:2, 4:1, each in a fraction f ∈ {0.1 … 1} of cells on a 1:1 background:
        L_exp = population-average copies / 2, h_exp = population-average major fraction (e.g. 1:0 in f: L = 1 − f/2, h = 1/(2 − f))
output  posterior over states; MAP state; major/minor relative copies with 90% bounds; 'no modelled state fits' if |z| > 2.5 for either modality

Assumptions: diploid 1:1 background; population-average mixtures (one event type per region); independent modality likelihoods (they share fragments); σ_A and τ from random windows, which can contain real events; attenuation k unknown (calls that change with k are unresolved). Why this method: it is the simplest way to make the user's question explicit (which haplotype, how many copies, in how many cells), keeps each modality's evidence visible (depth-only vs allele-only MAP columns), and refuses to force a call when nothing fits. Result: MYC and 5q fit a 2:1 gain with an identified major haplotype; the chr7 dips fit no state (depth 0.13–0.34× flanks with balanced alleles); 9p21 is not called a loss because LOH removes heterozygous sites from the selected SNP set (ascertainment), so balanced alleles there are not evidence against loss.

Cell-level joint mixture (exp 18)

What it does. Moves from pooled regions to individual cells: in each region every cell is either background (1:1) or carries one event type; the model estimates the fraction π of event cells, which cells they are, and whether depth and alleles agree.

cell i, region r:  state s_i ∈ {background, event e},  P(s_i = event) = π   (π ≥ 0.05 under the alternative)
depth   y_i | s ~ 0.99·NB2(μ_i·L_s, α_r) + 0.01·NB2(μ_i·L_s, 1)          (1% broad component: outlier-robust, a nuisance)
        μ_i = E_i · (n_target / n_outside) · exp(g_c) · exp(a_c)
        E_i = cell's raw total outside the region (exposure); g_c = cluster's local flank rate
        a_c = b + u_c = region baseline offset, b shared by all cells, u_c per cluster,
              with priors from uncalled random windows → a homogeneous accessibility shift is absorbed by b, not by π
allele  M_i | N_i, s ~ BetaBinomial(N_i, h_s, ρ_r / N_i)                     (N = AT on confidently oriented bins, M = major-haplotype counts, exp 16)
        h_0 = 0.5 + d0 (orientation folding bias, prior from random windows); h_event = h_0 + k (h_e − 0.5), k = 0.6 (1.0 sensitivity)
events  1:0, 0:0, 2:0, 2:1, 2:2, 4:1 (depth level L_e, major fraction h_e), each fitted separately
test    T = max over event types of 2 (ℓ_alt − ℓ_null); p from a hierarchical parametric bootstrap (B = 99) that redraws the baseline and
        folding offsets from their priors and refits the whole pipeline; profile CI for π
labels  clonal (π CI ≥ 0.8) · subclonal · small-fraction deviation (< 10% of cells) · no evidence · insufficient evidence

Assumptions: one event type per region; cells are independent given their state and cluster; the baseline offset prior from random windows is representative; the allele model conditions on N, so AT itself is not modelled (its dependence on depth is not double counted, but depth and allele still share fragments); orientation comes from the pooled 'all' cells, so a cluster with the opposite major haplotype is mis-oriented (cluster 4 on chr8q). Why this method: pooled regions cannot separate 'all cells slightly lower' (accessibility or clonal) from 'some cells much lower' (subclone); the per-cell likelihoods chosen in experiment 15 make that mixture identifiable in principle, and the jointly estimated baseline offset protects against the most common confounder. Calibration result: on 30 uncalled random windows, 7 (≈ 23%) are flagged; without the shared baseline offset 15/30; but 0/30 are supported by both modalities. So: both-modality cell-level calls are credible (MYC, 5q13-qter, chr8:115–145 Mb), depth-only cell-level calls are not (including the chr7 'subclonal' windows and the 9p21/19q 'clonal' calls), until the depth null includes the between-cell and between-cluster accessibility heterogeneity that random windows show.

Segmentation diagnostics and the spike-in benchmark (exp 05, 11, 13, 14, 19)

What the current AD segmentation is good at. Boundaries are reproducible between disjoint halves of the cells (split-half r > 0.99; all 10 saved-window edges that coincide with breakpoints reappear), and the select-then-test procedure accepted 1/300 first splits on exchangeable nulls (exp 05).

Where it fails. (1) Significance tests reproducible structure, not copy number. (2) In the benchmark, moderate focal changes are rarely segmented (figure). Diagnosis (exp 19): in a long segment the single best AD cut is elsewhere and is rejected, which ends the search (masking); a two-cut "segment vs rest" scan rescues deep losses but not one-copy changes, because inside their own segment natural windows differ from the rest as much as the injected change. (3) Count-likelihood segmentation with sampling-only dispersion produces ~4,200 segments; it needs a between-bin baseline-variance term (τ² ≈ 0.26 in the joint HMM; exp 11, exp 13).

Detection of injected events (both edges within ±2 bins and CN in the injected direction) by width. Circles: experiment 14, realistic thinning of raw counts in cluster 0 (f = 1; 248 realizations in total; Wilson 95% intervals). Squares: experiment 19, two-cut scan on clean edits (8 loci per point). Events narrower than 20 bins cannot be a segment by construction.

Toward a haplotype-aware CNV caller

Do the pieces make a haplotype-aware CNV caller? They make its skeleton, and on the controls it behaves sensibly; as stitched now it would not be a trustworthy caller, for three reasons: the segmentation is depth-driven and misses moderate focal events, depth has no baseline so depth-only calls are not calibrated, and the allele inputs are bin-summed with unverified phase. A design that follows from the evidence:

  1. Allele-first segmentation: a phase-switch-aware HMM over bins on the oriented major-haplotype fraction (experiment 16 is most of this), because allele imbalance is much less confounded by accessibility than depth (5q/1q/MYC imbalance is 3–6× the random-window null; depth contrasts of uncalled regions reach the size of one-copy changes).
  2. Depth as the second emission: NB2 with exposure and an explicit baseline-variance term, ideally against a baseline built from unrelated samples; depth gives the direction and magnitude of an allelic event and is the only evidence where alleles are missing.
  3. Allele-specific state space (experiment 17) with explicit outcomes: supported by both, depth-only (unconfirmed), allele-only (CN-LOH candidate), no modelled state fits, unobservable (QC-removed), and a site-density term so that LOH-driven loss of heterozygous sites counts as evidence for loss.
  4. Cell-level mixture (experiment 18) on top, for clonal vs subclonal fractions and mirrored events, after its depth null is calibrated to ≈ 5% on random windows.

This converges on the architecture of Numbat-multiome (haplotype-aware HMM with expression/accessibility and allele emissions). Once fragments and SNP-level phased allele counts exist, running Numbat-multiome ATAC-bin as the comparator is cheaper than rebuilding everything; what this review adds is the no-normal calibration (empirical regional nulls), the explicit 'no state fits / unobservable / depth-only' outcomes, and the QC-gap bookkeeping.

Open questions and the experiments that would resolve them

  • Are the chr7 0.13–0.34× plateaus mappability, accessibility or dosage? → same bins in ≥ 2 unrelated scATAC samples + a read-length-matched mappability track; decision: recurrence across samples with normal DNA → baseline offset.
  • How large is the true allelic attenuation (k)? → SNP-level, molecule-aware counts on a sample with DNA-defined allele-specific states.
  • Can depth-only cell mixtures be calibrated? → add between-cell/cluster accessibility heterogeneity to the null; target ≈ 5% on random windows.
  • Does allele-first segmentation recover one-copy focal events that the AD scan misses? → rerun experiment 14's injections with matching allele thinning.
  • What do QC-removed runs contain? → pre-QC counts / fragments.
  • Is cluster 4's opposite major haplotype on chr8q a mirrored event? → per-cell orientation within cluster 4 with SNP-level phase sets.

What the evidence supports

Supports

  • An evidence layer with modality-specific likelihoods and explicit 'no state fits / depth-only / unobservable' outcomes.
  • Allele-first, phase-aware segmentation as the core of a haplotype-aware caller.
  • Keeping QC-removed bins as an explicit state.
  • Both-modality cell-level clonal/subclonal estimates.

Does not support / cannot tell

  • Copy number at any saved window; whether the chr7 plateaus are dosage.
  • Depth-only subclonal calls (uncalibrated).
  • Claims beyond MCF10-A; any tool outperforming the baseline on real truth.
  • Verified parental haplotype identity (phase unverified).

Decision impact

Ordering of the plan (recommendation.md): 1 evidence layer (now prototyped), 2 QC bookkeeping, 3 cross-sample depth baseline + mappability, 4 allele-first segmentation with depth as second emission (replaces 'raw-count depth segmentation' as the segmentation priority), 5 SNP-level allele inputs; GC tuning and zero-inflation models are dropped from the plan.

Next checks

  • Obtain fragments + SNP-level phased allele counts; rerun pileup with molecule-aware counting.
  • Process ≥ 2 unrelated scATAC samples (one with DNA truth) with identical bins.
  • Prototype the allele-first HMM caller from experiments 16/13/17 and benchmark with experiment 14 injections (depth + allele thinning).
  • Run Numbat-multiome ATAC-bin as comparator when its inputs exist.
