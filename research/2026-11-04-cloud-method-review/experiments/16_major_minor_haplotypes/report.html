Bottom line

Exploratory: inferring the phase orientation of each bin with an HMM recovers most of the allelic imbalance that GT-oriented summing loses at 5q13-qter (|h-0.5| 0.010 naive to 0.100 oriented; phase-agnostic 0.109) and at MYC (0.032 to 0.087; phase-agnostic 0.106), little at 19q13-qter (0.002 to 0.013; phase-agnostic 0.049) and none at the 9p21 zone. Major/minor labels are inferred, not verified phase.

Key findings

  1. Orientation inference recovers the attenuated imbalance at strongly imbalanced loci. 5q13-qter: naive h 0.510 vs HMM h_hat 0.600 (95% CI 0.603-0.607), phase-agnostic delta 0.109 (recovered share 0.91); MYC: 0.532 vs 0.588 (CI 0.579-0.599), share 0.75.
  2. Weak or sparse loci are not rescued. 19q13-qter: 0.502 vs 0.512 (phase-agnostic delta 0.049, share 0.22; hazard x0.1 gives 0.500, rho=0 gives 0.525). The 9p21 zone: 0.529 vs 0.515 although the phase-agnostic delta is 0.186 (CI 0.000-0.238); 12 bins with AT>=5 and 1,486 AT in total.
  3. GT orientation flips often between bins. 5q13-qter needs 128 Viterbi orientation switches over about 111 Mb (1.15/Mb) against a prior of 0.20/Mb; 19 of 31 imbalanced `all` segments exceed the null 95th percentile of switch rate (4.0 per 10 Mb). Switch counts depend on the prespecified hazard (5q: 98 at x0.1, 194 at x10).
  4. Null calibration. In 60 uncalled random 25-bin windows (all cells) delta_hat has median 0.010, 95th percentile 0.035 (max 0.058); 31 of 164 eligible `all` segments exceed it (clusters: 0: 25, 1: 26, 2: 26, 3: 24, 4: 19). Null windows are 25 bins, longer segments are less noisy, so the threshold is conservative for them.
  5. Cross-cluster orientation is mostly the same haplotype. Across 91 (imbalanced all-segment x cluster) comparisons with >=10 confident bins, 84 are the same major haplotype, 4 mixed and 3 opposite; the 3 opposite cases are all cluster 4 on chr8:115-145 Mb (agreement 0.06-0.15), a candidate mirrored event in 182 cells (hypothesis). 64 comparisons had too few bins.
  6. Per-cell fractions are not binomially homogeneous. Across imbalanced segments the median dispersion phi is 1.62 vs 1.03 expected for beta-binomial cells with rho = 0.0038; 113/120 segment-cluster rows exceed the simulated 95th percentile. At chr8:115-134 Mb 69% of cluster-4 cells have major fraction < 0.5 (oriented by the pooled call) vs about 17-20% in clusters 0-3.
  7. Saved chr7 windows. chr7:8.3-10.7 and 70.0-73.3 Mb show a small oriented imbalance (delta_hat 0.048, 0.043; null 95th pct 0.035) though their phase-agnostic delta is 0.000; 51.4-53.5 and 67.4-70.0 Mb are 0.000. This is weak, near the null edge, and far below the 5q/MYC values.
  8. Sensitivity. Number of imbalanced `all` segments (each setting with its own null): base 31, hazard x0.1 26, x10 24, rho=0 19, rho=0.02 30; Spearman of delta_hat vs base 0.71-0.86. Large effects (5q, MYC, chr1q) are stable; small effects are not.

Question and why it matters

Allele-specific relative copy number needs, for each region with allelic imbalance, which haplotype carries the extra (or the retained) copy. Experiment 06 showed that summing HB over bins in GT orientation attenuates imbalance strongly (the published MYC amplification gives an effective |dBAF| of about 0.106 phase-agnostically, but the GT-oriented sum shows far less) because the GT phase between bins is not verified and the package has no phase-set IDs. Numbat (Gao et al. 2023) handles this with a haplotype-orientation HMM; this experiment applies the same idea at 100 kb-bin resolution to these data and asks (i) how well the orientation and the major-haplotype fraction are recovered, (ii) how much of the attenuation at the published MCF10-A gains is recovered, (iii) whether the same haplotype is major in every cluster, and (iv) what the null distribution looks like. It is an exploratory diagnostic, not a validated phasing method.

Inputs

  • HA / HB / AT parquet (bins x barcodes) joined by barcode name to the AnnData var / obs; checks: {"coords_match_anndata": true, "barcode_set_equal": true, "AT_equals_HA_plus_HB": true, "nonnegative": true, "n_nonzero_AT_entries": 8026284}.
  • Saved segmentations: karyogram_cluster_segments.tsv, groups all and cluster0-4 (955 segments).
  • WNN clusters via common.cell_clusters (sizes 1286 / 833 / 603 / 556 / 182).
  • Regions: the seven part == 'target' windows of research/2026-10-29-low-coverage/shared/regions.tsv (six candidate windows plus the historical chr9 70-100 Mb comparison, as in exp 04) and the four literature loci of exp 04.
  • Null: first 60 of the 226 random windows (of 300, exp 04 rule, seed (20261104, 4)) with no called bin in the `all` segmentation.
  • rho = 0.00384 from exp 06 summary.json; hazard lambda = 1 switch / 5 Mb (prespecified, not fitted).

Method

Observed per segment (cells pooled): for each informative bin j (AT_j > 0): k_j = HB_j, n_j = AT_j = HA_j + HB_j.
Hidden orientation o_j in {B, A}; B-major means haplotype B is the major haplotype in bin j.
Emission:  k_j | n_j, o_j = B ~ BetaBinomial(n_j, p = 0.5 + delta, rho)      o_j = A:  p = 0.5 - delta
           BetaBinomial(k; n, a, b) with a = p c, b = (1-p) c, c = (1 - rho)/rho,  rho = 0.0038 (exp 06; rho = 0 means Binomial)
Transition between consecutive informative bins at base-pair distance d_j (bin start to bin start):
           s_j = P(o_{j+1} != o_j) = 0.5 (1 - exp(-2 * lambda * d_j)),   lambda = 1 / 5 Mb  (prespecified)
           sensitivity: lambda x 0.1 and x 10.   Initial state 1/2, 1/2.
Likelihood: L(delta) = sum over orientation paths of  prod_j P(k_j | n_j, o_j; delta) prod_j T(o_j, o_{j+1}; d_j)   (scaled forward algorithm)
Estimate:   delta_hat = argmax_{delta in [0, 0.49]} L(delta)  (profile likelihood on a grid, step 0.0025); h_hat = 0.5 + delta_hat
            no EM is needed: delta is one parameter and the grid is exhaustive.
Posterior:  P(o_j = B | data; delta_hat) by forward-backward; expected switches = sum_j P(o_j != o_{j+1} | data);
            Viterbi path for the switch count.
Cell bootstrap: resample cells with replacement (multinomial weights, seed (20261104,16,stream,group,item)), recompute k_j, n_j, refit
            delta on the 0.01 grid with parabolic refinement; 95% percentile interval.
Naive:      f = sum HB / sum AT;  h_naive = max(f, 1-f).
Phase-agnostic (exp 06):  delta_eff = sqrt(max(0, mean_j [ ((2k_j - n_j)^2 / n_j - (1 + (n_j - 1) rho)) / (4 n_j) ]))  over bins with n_j >= 5.
Imbalanced:  delta_hat > 95th percentile of delta_hat over 60 uncalled random 25-bin windows (same cell set).
Orientation labels are anchored to the HB column (GT phase), so P(B-major) can be compared between cell groups on the same bin.

Segments need bins_AT_ge5 >= 8 to be fitted; the others are reported as not eligible. The same pipeline runs on regions (all cells and each cluster) and on random windows. The hazard is a prior chosen before looking at results; because the observed switch counts are far above it, the x10 sensitivity is informative.

Orientation and switches

How to read: left, the GT-oriented HB/AT of each bin (AT >= 5), coloured by side of 0.5; middle, the same bins after HMM orientation (fraction on the inferred major haplotype), with the posterior P(B-major) in red; right, the two distributions. In imbalanced segments the GT-oriented values are bimodal and mirror-symmetric around 0.5 (for chr1:166.8-236 Mb about half the bins near 0.29 and half near 0.71), in runs of a few bins: the GT phase label switches between runs, so the pooled GT sum cancels towards 0.5. The HMM flips the runs where B is the minor allele, which moves every bin to the same side. That is only meaningful where the per-bin deviation is far above sampling noise and mirror-symmetric; in uncalled random windows the same procedure gives |h - 0.5| <= 0.035 (null 95th percentile), which bounds the folding bias.

Limits and alternative explanations

  • Allele-specific accessibility / mapping bias. HB / HA are chromatin accessibility reads; allele-specific accessibility (imprinting, regulatory variants) or reference mapping bias gives a haplotype excess without copy-number change. A consistent orientation would look the same.
  • Copy-number-neutral LOH and loss. Imbalance does not say gain or loss; depth is needed for that. In loss or LOH regions heterozygous sites are poorly ascertained (AT per fragment is lowest at 9p21 in exp 06), so the allele fraction there is a biased sample.
  • No SNP-level phase sets. Phase is the sum over selected SNPs per bin; the HMM works on bin-level orientation. Observed switch rates (about 1 per Mb at 5q) are much higher than the prior; a model with SNP-level phasing would be more accurate.
  • Repeated site counting. AT sums SNP-site DP, not unique fragments; the 0.0038 rho only partly absorbs it. h_hat could be attenuated or intervals too narrow; cell-bootstrap CIs do not include bin-level model error.
  • Constant-delta assumption. One delta per segment; heterogeneous imbalance inside a segment, subclones, or segments mixing different events are averaged.
  • Boundary and threshold. delta_hat >= 0 and the 25-bin null make small-segment calls near the threshold uncertain; the null has only 60 windows.
  • Orientation labels are inferred. Major/minor is a statement about count asymmetry; haplotype identity across segments is not verified (GT phase, no phase sets).
  • Per-cell orientation uses the pooled call and so is biased towards the major side.

What the evidence supports

Supports

  • Orientation inference raises the estimated imbalance at 5q13-qter and MYC by roughly a factor 10 and 3 relative to the GT-oriented sum, close to the phase-agnostic estimate.
  • Large imbalanced segments (chr1q, chr5q, chr8q) are oriented the same way in all clusters with enough data.
  • A cluster-4-specific opposite orientation at chr8:115-145 Mb is a candidate for a mirrored (hypothesis) or cluster-specific allelic event.
  • GT bin orientation is not stable over Mb distances in this data (switch counts far above the prior).

Does not support / cannot tell

  • Verified haplotype identity, phase blocks, or allele-specific copy number; depth was not used.
  • Any statement that the imbalance is a copy-number change (allele-specific accessibility and mapping bias cannot be excluded).
  • Recovery at weakly imbalanced or sparse loci: 19q13-qter is only partly recovered and 9p21 is not, and the chr7 window values are within about 1.3x the null threshold.
  • A calibrated significance test: the null has 60 windows of 25 bins and delta_hat is boundary-biased.
  • Mirrored events as fact: cluster 4 is 182 cells and the opposite call involves three adjacent chr8 segments.

Decision impact

recommendation.md (rank 5, joint depth-allele likelihood) assumed that allelic evidence is available as a signed BAF. In these data the GT-oriented sum is attenuated (5q13-qter naive |h-0.5| 0.010), so a joint method needs an explicit latent phase-orientation state, as in Numbat, before depth and allele evidence are combined. This result supports adding that state to the evidence layer (and to experiment 13's joint HMM) but not adopting any particular major/minor call as a CN statement. Cluster-specific orientation matters: pooled `all` orientation hides opposite haplotypes in a subcluster (chr8q, cluster 4), so allele-specific CN should be estimated per cluster and compared, not only for `all`. No scientific behaviour of the existing pipeline is changed by this experiment.

Next checks

  • Run the orientation HMM jointly with the depth track (experiment 13) so that depth can break ties between gain and loss at imbalanced segments.
  • Replace bin-level HB by SNP-level counts with local phase blocks (or read-backed phasing) and compare switch counts with the prior.
  • Validate on a sample with known phase (or a matched normal / bulk WGS BAF) before calling any haplotype major in a published locus.
  • Test the cluster-4 chr8q opposite orientation by splitting cluster 4 and by an independent pseudobulk of allele counts; check allele-specific accessibility at heterozygous sites in that interval.
  • Replace the constant-delta assumption by a per-bin state (e.g. 0, delta1, delta2) and use a larger, size-matched null (>= 300 windows per segment length class).
