Bottom line

The four chr7 saved windows hold 0.13–0.34x their flanks in raw counts, below the 0.5x floor of a one-copy loss, independent of cell coverage and shared across clusters, while uncalled random windows alone span 0.44–1.72x; this fits a shared regional baseline effect (possibly with a smaller dosage change) better than a simple clonal one-copy loss, and does not establish copy number.

Key findings

  1. Region families: 7 saved windows (6 loss windows + chr9 70–100 Mb comparison), 67 short called segments (46 loss, 21 gain; n_bins ≤ 100, all-source karyogram), 4 literature loci, 300 random 25-bin windows (seed [20261104, 4]).
  2. chr7 saved windows: raw rate / flank = 0.20, 0.16, 0.13, 0.34 (processed X: 0.36, 0.27, 0.18, 0.44), all < 0.5; the null percentile of |log rr| is 97–100%.
  3. Uncalled random windows (226 of 300) range 0.437–1.715x (5th–95th percentile, median 0.973); 19 of them fall below 0.5x. 26 random windows are below 0.5x, of which 6 are majority-called loss.
  4. Coverage invariance: |log(rr low-coverage / rr high-coverage)| ≤ 0.028 for the chr7 windows (≤ 0.028 for all saved windows with flanks); cluster-specific ratios stay within 0.11–0.41 for chr7.
  5. Among cells expected to have ≥ 5 target fragments, at most 2.3% observe zero in the chr7 windows (bound on the share of cells compatible with near-complete loss); raw zero-cell counts exceed the homogeneous expectation (e.g. 152 observed vs 34 expected in chr7_67.4_70.0).
  6. Dispersion: cluster-adjusted phi is 1.92–2.73 for chr7 windows (95–100th percentile of random windows), but in random windows phi rises with contrast size (correlation of |log rr| with log phi = 0.67), so elevated phi is not by itself evidence of a subclonal mixture.
  7. Called short segments: 30 of 46 called losses fall below the 0.5x floor (median rr 0.36); called gains have median rr 1.84.
  8. Literature loci relative to genome-wide density (raw / processed X): MYC 8q24 2.57 / 2.34; 19q13-qter 1.82 / 0.93; 9p21 zone 0.41 / 0.64; 5q13-qter 1.14 / 1.24.

Question and why it matters

Question. For fixed regions, how deep is the raw-count depletion or excess, is it shared by all cells (homogeneous) or concentrated in some (mixture), does it depend on cell coverage or cluster, and which explanations does its size allow?

Why it matters. The recommendation’s central uncertainty is whether the chr7 dips behave like a simple clonal or subclonal one-copy loss, and whether raw depth contrasts at this scale carry dosage information at all. This experiment supplies the numbers for the per-region evidence layer (depth contrast against random windows, coverage dependence, compatibility with a one-copy change, per-cell mixture bound) and for the stage-2 argument that a cross-sample baseline is the decisive missing experiment.

Inputs

  • Matrix. count_matrix.h5ad (3,460 cells × 25,337 bins): layers/raw (pre-GC counts, used for all primary statistics) and X (processed; secondary). Units: fragments per 100 kb bin.
  • Clusters. WNN clusters (wnn_leiden_0.3, 5 clusters) from integrated.h5mu; coverage terciles by raw total per cell.
  • Region families (defined by rule before computing any statistic): saved = the 6 saved chr7/chr9 windows plus the historical chr9 70–100 Mb comparison, with frozen 50-retained-bin flanks on each side (shared regions.tsv/region_bins.tsv); called = every segment of the all source in the saved genome-pool karyogram with n_bins ≤ 100 and cn_call ≠ 1 (46 loss/weak loss, 21 gain/weak gain; flanks up to 50 retained bins each side within the chromosome); literature = four published MCF10-A loci (9p21 hemizygous zone chr9:20–24 Mb approximate, MYC chr8:127.0–128.5 Mb, 19q13-qter chr19 ≥ 35 Mb, 5q13-qter chr5 ≥ 70 Mb), compared with the genome-wide density only (no flanks); random = 300 random windows of 25 consecutive retained bins without internal gap (same flank rule). Random windows are not neutral truth: they can contain events.
  • 300-cell panel. Frozen shared panel (cells.tsv) used for a consistency column.
  • Not available. Independent CN truth, mappability, cross-sample baselines, SNP-level allele data (experiment 06 uses the allelic matrices).

Method

  1. For every region sum raw counts per cell over target bins and over flank bins (all 3,460 cells).
  2. Rate ratio rr_flank is a ratio of sums, so cell exposure cancels. rr_genome compares target density to genome-wide density.
  3. Repeat rr in raw-coverage terciles and WNN clusters; coverage_log_slope = log(rr_low / rr_high).
  4. Per-cell dispersion phi: Pearson dispersion of the target count given the cell’s target+flank total around one common binomial proportion; phi_cluster uses a proportion per cluster (5 degrees of freedom removed).
  5. Zero test: cells with zero target counts versus the homogeneous binomial expectation; among cells expected to have ≥ 5 target fragments, the fraction observing zero (an upper bound on the share of cells that could carry a near-complete loss).
  6. Compare every statistic with the null distribution of the random windows (percentiles).
rr_flank   = (sum_cells target / n_target_bins) / (sum_cells flank / n_flank_bins)
rr_genome  = (sum target / n_target_bins) / (total counts / n_bins)
n_c = T_c + F_c;  pi = sum T / sum n
phi       = sum_c (T_c - n_c pi)^2 / (n_c pi (1 - pi)) / (n_cells - 1)
lam_c     = n_c * pi;  frac_zero = #{T_c = 0 and lam_c >= 5} / #{lam_c >= 5}
implied_loss_fraction_if_1copy = min(1, 2 (1 - rr_flank))   # share of cells with one-copy loss that would give rr

Dosage rule: rr_flank < 0.5 cannot arise from a one-copy loss of a diploid baseline with unchanged accessibility/mappability alone. Random windows: seed tuple (20261104, 4), numpy PCG64 (default_rng); candidates are 25-bin runs with no internal gap on every chromosome. Percentiles are of the random-window distribution.

Sensitivity

Checks included: 300-cell panel versus all cells (rr_flank_raw_panel300), raw versus processed X, coverage terciles and clusters. Not varied: the 50-bin flank width, the 25-bin random-window width and the 5-fragment threshold of the zero test (a ±20% width sensitivity is a recommended next check); the percentile of a saved window depends on the random-window distribution, which includes real events.

Limits and alternative explanations

  • Random windows are not neutral truth; they sample the same genome that contains the events, so null percentiles are calibration, not proof.
  • The one-copy floor assumes a diploid baseline and unchanged accessibility/mappability; a ratio below 0.5 is also expected from a regional baseline offset (accessibility or mappability), possibly combined with a smaller dosage change. These cannot be separated without a cross-sample baseline or mappability track.
  • Homogeneity (cluster-invariant ratios, bounded zero fractions) argues against a small subclone carrying a deep loss but cannot distinguish a shared baseline from a shared clonal change.
  • Literature loci are approximate and compared with genome-wide density only; the 9p21 extent is a ~4 Mb approximation.
  • Called segments are outputs of the same pipeline, so ‘called’ windows are not independent evidence.

What the evidence supports

Supports

  • The chr7 saved windows are coverage-invariant, similar in every cluster and have few zero-count cells among informative cells, which is consistent with a shared regional depth deficit rather than a small deep-loss subclone.
  • Their raw ratios are below the 0.5x floor of a one-copy loss from a diploid baseline with unchanged accessibility, so a one-copy loss alone does not account for the depth.
  • Depth contrasts of one-copy size are common in uncalled random windows, so depth alone is weak evidence of dosage.

Does not support / cannot tell

  • The copy-number state of any saved window.
  • Whether the chr7 plateaus are mappability, accessibility or dosage (no cross-sample baseline or mappability track).
  • That elevated dispersion indicates subclonal mixture: random deep windows show the same association.

Decision impact

Supports recommendation ranks 1 and 3: report raw-count contrast with its percentile among same-width random windows, the coverage-invariance and one-copy-floor labels, and obtain a cross-sample baseline and mappability before changing any formula. It argues against reading the chr7 dips as simple clonal one-copy losses, but does not rule out a smaller dosage change on top of a shared baseline.

Next checks

  • Run the same binning and QC on unrelated samples, with a read-length-matched mappability track, to test whether the chr7 plateaus recur.
  • Repeat with 25±20% window widths and different flank widths to test label stability.
  • Add an independent DNA-based copy-number measurement for MCF10-A (or a sample with truth) to calibrate the one-copy floor.
