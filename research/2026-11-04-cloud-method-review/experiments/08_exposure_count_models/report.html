Bottom line

Raw-count models with exposure fit the data's sampling behaviour (NB explains the zeros), but they only become informative when judged against the spread of same-width uncalled regions. On that scale the deep chr7 windows stand out and the chr9 windows do not.

Key findings

  1. Sampling-based tests call almost everything: |z| > 1.96 under quasi-Poisson for 98% of random windows and 98% of called losses. Quasi-Poisson φ ≈ 2.6 and NB α ≈ 0.011 fix sampling variance only.
  2. Against the empirical spread of 226 uncalled random windows (median log rr -0.027, robust SD 0.391), 27/46 called short losses and 4/21 called short gains remain unusual (BH q < 0.05).
  3. The four chr7 saved windows: rate ratio 0.13–0.34, empirical z -5.2 to -2.7. The chr9 windows (z -0.7, -1.7) sit within the random range.
  4. Held-out zeros: in chr7 8.3–10.7, 30 test cells have zero target counts; Poisson predicts 2.5 and NB 23.9. NB accounts for the zeros of the deep chr7 windows, so zero inflation is not required there (E:3 precondition not met).
  5. Cluster heterogeneity is common: part × cluster p < 0.01 in 96% of called losses and 86% of random windows. With thousands of cells, small between-cluster differences are detectable.
  6. Coverage dependence (a technical signature) appears in 59% of called losses vs 25% of random windows.

Question and why it matters

Method record E:1 (2026-10-29 review): model each cell's raw counts in a region with its library exposure, compare Poisson, quasi-Poisson and negative-binomial variance, and check whether zeros and spread are explained. The decision it informs: can a raw-count, exposure-aware likelihood join the evidence layer recommended in recommendation.md, and what null does it need?

Inputs

  • layers/raw of the AnnData: 3,460 cells × 25,337 retained bins (post-QC, pre-GC integer counts).
  • WNN clusters wnn_leiden_0.3 (5 clusters) from the MuData.
  • Regions from experiment 04's rule: 7 saved windows, 67 called short segments, 4 literature loci with no flank (excluded here), 300 random windows (seed (20261104, 4)), 226 of them uncalled.
  • Not available: pre-QC counts, mappability, independent truth.

Method

for region r, cell i, part p ∈ {target, flank}:
  y_ip ~ F(μ_ip)
  log μ_ip = log E_i + log n_p + b0 + b1·[p = target] + γ_cluster(i)
  E_i = cell raw total minus target and flank counts;  n_p = retained bins in part p
  F: Poisson | quasi-Poisson (scale = Pearson χ²/df) | NB2 (α by Cameron–Trivedi) | Poisson + sandwich SE clustered by cell
heterogeneity: add part×coverage-tercile or part×cluster terms; quasi-Poisson Wald/F test
held-out check: split cells within cluster (seed (20261104, 8)); fit y_it on training cells (target only);
  predict P(y=0) on test cells: Poisson exp(−μ), NB (1+αμ)^(−1/α)
empirical null: median and MAD of b1 over uncalled random windows → z_empirical; BH over called segments

Sensitivity

All four variance models give nearly the same point estimate; the intervals differ by φ but stay narrow. Rate ratios on the frozen 300-cell panel agree with all cells (column rr_panel300 in region_models.tsv). Cluster adjustment is included in every model; removing it was not prespecified.

Limits and alternative explanations

  • Accessibility, mappability and CN are confounded in every column; a deep, homogeneous, NB-consistent depletion is equally expected from an inaccessible or poorly mappable region.
  • Called segments were selected on these data, so their empirical p-values are conditional on selection.
  • Random windows are not neutral truth; some contain events, which widens the reference and makes the test conservative.
  • Exposure excludes the focal region and flanks but still contains other events; cluster terms can absorb real signal.

What the evidence supports

Supports

  • NB, not zero inflation, is adequate for zeros in the deep chr7 windows.
  • An empirical, region-based null is required; sampling-based p-values are not.
  • 27 called short losses and 4 called gains are unusual relative to uncalled regions.

Does not support / cannot tell

  • Any statement that a depletion is dosage rather than accessibility or mappability.
  • Calibrated discovery claims for called segments (selected on the same data).
  • Absolute CN states.

Decision impact

E:1 can enter the evidence layer as rate ratio + empirical z + heterogeneity flags, with NB as the sampling model. It does not change the recommendation's ordering: the empirical z is only as good as the reference regions, which strengthens the case for a cross-sample technical baseline (stage 2).

Next checks

  • Replace random windows with a cross-sample baseline (same bins in unrelated samples) and recompute z.
  • Repeat with region selection and assessment on disjoint cell halves to remove selection bias.
  • Add mappability/GC covariates once a matched mappability track is available.
