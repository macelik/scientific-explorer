Bottom line

Moderate focal changes are missed mainly because the binary scan's single best cut in a long segment lies elsewhere and is rejected, which ends the search there; a segment-vs-rest (two-cut) scan inside the same segment is the targeted fix to test.

Key findings

  1. Baseline AD binary scan, clean clonal one-copy loss (×0.5): detected 12% / 12% / 14% at 20/30/50 bins; two-cut scan 0% / 0% / 0%.
  2. Gain (×1.5): baseline 0% / 0% / 0%; two-cut 0% / 0% / 0%.
  3. Deep loss (×0.1): baseline 57% / 43% / 86%; two-cut 29% / 0% / 0%.
  4. Why the baseline misses: accepted cut elsewhere; recursion ended (depth/geometry) (16); top cut at event; rejected by local test (1); top cut elsewhere; rejected by global test (8); top cut elsewhere; rejected by local test (24).
  5. Two-cut threshold from 45 unmodified cluster-0 segments (150–1,500 bins): 7% of them exceed it by construction (≈5%).
  6. n = 8 loci per cell; intervals are wide (Wilson 95% for 0/8 is 0–32%, for 8/8 is 68–100%).

Question and why it matters

Experiment 14 and an independent check showed the workbench AD scan rarely segments a clean 0.5× or 1.5× change of 20–50 bins in a 1,286-cell pseudobulk. If the cause is the top-down search rather than the data, the next change belongs in segmentation (recommendation stage 4), and it transfers to every sample.

Inputs

  • Saved cluster-0 processed-X pseudobulk (1,286 cells), saved cluster-0 genome-pool breakpoints.
  • Event loci: the first 8 prespecified loci per (type, width) of experiment 14 (f = 1).
  • Events are clean multiplicative edits of the pseudobulk: idealised, no sampling noise added, no GC re-correction.

Method

  Events: clean multiplicative edits of the saved cluster-0 processed-X pseudobulk (no GC recomputation; an
  idealised version of exp 14's thinning), at the first 8 loci of experiment 14's prespecified locus lists
  (same scenario seeds, read from 14's realizations.tsv where present, else drawn with 14's rule),
  types loss_one (x0.5), gain (x1.5), loss_deep (x0.1); widths 20, 30, 50; f = 1 only (pseudobulk factor
  1 - f/2 etc. is equivalent for a mean).
  A. Baseline: workbench scan (server.allelic_scan.scan_chromosome, DEFAULTS, genome pool). For missed edges,
     classify the reason from the audit: the deepest tested node containing the event; was its top
     geometry-valid candidate elsewhere and rejected by the local test, by the global test, or did recursion
     stop for geometry/depth.
  B. Two-cut AD scan ('segment vs rest', the idea behind circular binary segmentation): inside the final
     baseline segment that contains the event, for every window [i, i+w) with 20 <= w <= 120 bins that leaves
     >= 20 bins outside, statistic T(i, w) = AD(window, rest of segment) with the pinned pyEpi dist_ad.
     Detection: max T over the segment exceeds a threshold AND the argmax window's edges are within +-2 bins
     of the injected edges. Threshold: 95th percentile of the same max statistic over unmodified final
     cluster-0 segments of length 150-1,500 bins (all such segments; real background, so it includes any real
     events and is conservative), scaled per segment length by fitting max T ~ a + b log(n).
  C. False positives: fraction of the unmodified segments above the threshold (5% by construction overall;
     reported by length) and, for the injected chromosomes, any new window called away from the event.

Limits and alternative explanations

  • Idealised events (exact multiplicative change of the mean profile, no extra sampling noise, no GC re-correction); experiment 14 has the realistic thinning version.
  • The two-cut threshold comes from real segments that can contain real events, so it is conservative; it is not a calibrated genome-wide FDR.
  • The two-cut scan here is applied inside the baseline's final segment; a full method would integrate it into the recursion (e.g. circular binary segmentation or seeded/wild binary segmentation) and needs its own multiplicity control.
  • Detectability is not interpretation: a recovered boundary still needs the evidence layer (experiments 08, 17).

What the evidence supports

Supports

  • A segmentation change (two-cut/CBS-like or seeded binary segmentation) as the next method experiment.
  • Reporting 'not segmented' separately from 'no change' for focal events below ~0.5x contrast.

Does not support / cannot tell

  • Any claim about real events in MCF10-A; events here are synthetic.
  • A calibrated false-discovery rate for the two-cut scan.

Decision impact

Moves part of rank 4 (segmentation) forward: the binary AD recursion has a structural sensitivity gap for moderate focal events, independent of preprocessing. A two-cut or seeded scan should be benchmarked with exp 14's realistic injections and an FDR-controlled threshold before adoption.

Next checks

  • Integrate a two-cut statistic into the recursion and rerun experiment 14's realistic injections.
  • Calibrate the two-cut threshold by permuting cells between halves (sampling-only null) plus the background-segment null.
  • Compare with an exposure-aware count changepoint with a baseline-variance term (experiment 11 showed sampling-only dispersion over-segments).
