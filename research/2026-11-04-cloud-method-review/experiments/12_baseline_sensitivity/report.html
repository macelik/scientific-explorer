Bottom line

Under eight prespecified reference-free baselines the saved chr7/chr9 depletions stay below the 0.683 loss cut-off in 5 of 6 windows for all seven level baselines (chr9_20_27 in 6 of 7), but loss/gain classes of ordinary and random regions change with baseline choice often enough that a call is, consistent with the data, a statement about the chosen reference as much as about copy number.

Key findings

  1. Reversal (raw, loose; regions called by at least one baseline but not all seven): called losses 14/42, called gains 10/26, random-window losses 86/94 and gains 158/209; saved losses 1/6; literature loci loss 1/1, gain 2/3.
  2. Opposite-sign reversals (loss under one baseline, gain under another): 1 of 67 called segments but 31 of 300 random windows (raw, loose).
  3. Processed-X estimand is more stable than the raw rate: called losses 6/46, called gains 3/21; strict thresholds are less stable: called losses 17/31, gains 19/25.
  4. Saved windows (raw level, baseline a-f,h): chr7_51.4_53.5: 7/7 loss, range 0.13-0.23; chr7_67.4_70.0: 7/7 loss, range 0.10-0.14; chr7_70.0_73.3: 7/7 loss, range 0.30-0.47; chr7_8.3_10.7: 7/7 loss, range 0.17-0.43; chr9_20_27: 6/7 loss, range 0.41-0.84; chr9_22.5_26.6: 7/7 loss, range 0.27-0.60; chr9_70_100: 0/7 loss, range 0.93-1.31.
  5. The GC-decile baseline (h) is the main outlier for the chr7_8.3 and chr9 windows (e.g. chr9_20_27 = 0.84 vs 0.42-0.59 for the others), consistent with part of that depletion being GC-correlated.
  6. Cluster-vs-rest: chr9 windows have ratios 0.86-1.10 in every cluster (consistent with a depletion shared across clusters); chr7_8.3, chr7_51.4 and chr7_67.4 sit near or just above the random-window 95th percentile (0.48 max |log2|) driven by cluster 2 (1.36-1.53). Called losses: 4/46 cluster-specific, 32/46 below 0.85 in every cluster.
  7. Flags (descriptive Fisher): called losses near a QC-removed bin 24/46 vs 7/300 random windows (OR 46); near blacklist 26/46 vs 47/300. Caveat: depleted bins are the ones the nonzero-frequency filter removes, so this is consistent with a selection effect on the same signal, not an independent artifact test.
  8. Deep random windows (raw level c < 0.683) are not enriched near removed bins or blacklist, but are GC-extreme more often (18/66 vs 13/234).

Question and why it matters

A loss or gain call from pyEpiAneufinder is a threshold on (segment mean) / (a genome-wide scale), and experiment 03 showed the scale sits at the lower grid edge. This experiment asks how much each region's relative level, and the class it receives under fixed cut-offs, depends on which reference-free baseline is used.

  • C:3 reference-free relative baseline sensitivity: compare genome-wide centering, robust baselines and held-out alternatives; expose the baseline choice.
  • C:1 independent internal controls: none exist in this sample, so no normal cluster is forced from MCF10-A identity or apparent flatness and no baseline is treated as copy-neutral.
  • F:2 blacklist / genomic gap mask and F:4 GC content: technical context reported as soft flags, not as corrections.

Decision informed: whether the method recommendation should report calls as baseline-conditional relative levels (with the baseline exposed) rather than absolute copy-number states, and how much weight the saved chr7/chr9 depletions can carry.

Limits and alternative explanations

  • Segmentation is fixed; baselines are not propagated into re-segmentation.
  • Baselines a-e share one genome-wide distribution, so their agreement is not independent evidence; on a skewed, aneuploid genome they can all be off together.
  • f removes only one chromosome; h removes GC-driven structure but also any real GC-correlated copy-number signal.
  • Random windows are not neutral controls; called regions are selected by their own call, which biases overlap with c.
  • Alternative explanations for the saved depletions that this experiment cannot exclude: shared accessibility/mappability baseline offset, a GC-related effect, or real loss. Cluster-vs-rest cancels a shared baseline and so cannot distinguish them.
  • Bootstrap resamples cells only (300 reps) and ignores segmentation uncertainty. Enrichment tests are uncorrected and regions are correlated.

What the evidence supports

Supports

  • 5 of 6 saved loss windows are below the loose loss cut-off under all seven level baselines, consistent with a robust relative depletion regardless of centering.
  • chr9 windows' depletion is similar across clusters (cluster-vs-rest 0.86-1.10), consistent with a shared rather than cluster-specific pattern.
  • A call is partly a statement about the chosen baseline: many random and gain-class regions flip class across baselines.

Does not support / cannot tell

  • That any baseline is copy-neutral, or that the depletions are copy-number loss rather than a shared depth/accessibility baseline effect.
  • That GC is the cause of the chr9_20_27 shift; baseline h only shows GC-matched centering moves it.
  • That QC-removed-bin enrichment of called losses is an independent artifact; it is consistent with filter selection on depleted bins.

Decision impact

Supports the point already in recommendation.md (rank 2 cross-sample technical baseline, rank 4 baseline-offset model): relative depth levels should be reported with the baseline exposed, and loss/gain states, especially gains and strict thresholds, should be treated as baseline-conditional. It does not change the ranking; it adds a concrete reversal rate (called gains 10/26, random-window gains 158/209) to the evidence for needing an independent baseline.

Next checks

  • Run the same baselines on unrelated samples with DNA truth or an annotated normal population (recommendation rank 2) to see which baseline tracks truth.
  • Re-segment under baseline-offset alternatives and compare breakpoints rather than only classes.
  • Replace the 1 Mb removed-bin flag with a pre-filter depth flag to separate selection from artifact.
  • Check chr9_20_27 and chr7_8.3 with a LOESS GC correction versus the decile reference.
