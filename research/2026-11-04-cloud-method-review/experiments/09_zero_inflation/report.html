Bottom line

Exploratory: the bootstrap LRT nominally favours a zero component in 41/58 regions, including 23/30 negative windows, but with pi about 1 cell in 3460 (median 0.0003); this is isolated zero cells, not population zero inflation. NB removes most of the Poisson misfit, and the deep chr7 windows do not need a zero component.

Key findings

  1. Bootstrap NB vs ZINB LRT (B=199, both models refitted in every replicate): 41/58 regions have p < 0.05 (3/7 saved, 15/21 NB-misfit flagged, 23/30 negative windows); 41 survive BH q < 0.05 (smallest attainable p = 0.0050). Under a calibrated test ~5% of the negatives would be below 0.05.
  2. Saved windows: p_boot = chr7:8.3_10.7 0.195, chr7:51.4_53.5 1.000, chr7:67.4_70.0 0.100, chr7:70.0_73.3 0.015, chr9:20_27 1.000, chr9:22.5_26.6 0.005, chr9:70_100 0.005. The four deep chr7 windows have LR ≤ 1.4 and BIC favours NB over ZINB in all of them (dBIC 6.8 to 8.2); no held-out gain from ZINB in 4/4.
  3. Effect size is tiny: 36/41 supported regions have at most 3 observed zero cells in 3460 cells, where NB expects about 0 (median NB-expected zeros 4.0e-07); the supported pi is about 1-3 cells (median expected structural zeros 1.0, max 10.7). So the LRT is detecting isolated zero cells that the NB tail cannot accommodate (a zero where mu is large costs the NB tens of log units), not a population-level zero-inflation.
  4. Estimated constant pi is small: median 0.0003, max 0.0032 (chr7_67.4_70.0); 13/58 observed fits sit on the pi = 0 boundary and 96% of null replicates do, so null LR* is 0 in most replicates: the p-value floor and ties dominate, and a chi-square LRT would be invalid.
  5. Of the 41 nominally supported regions, 30 also gain held-out log score under ZINB over NB (positive = better); hurdle NB beats NB held-out in 30/58 regions and BIC prefers ZINB over NB in 37/58.
  6. Poisson vs NB: median ΔAIC (Poisson − NB) = 1360; NB wins by >10 AIC in 58/58 regions, so overdispersion, not zeros, is the dominant departure from Poisson (consistent with experiment 08).
  7. Where are the extra zeros? Summed over the 41 supported regions, observed-minus-NB-expected zero cells are 38 in the lowest cell-total tercile, 36 in the middle and 1 in the highest; almost none sits in the deepest third of cells, so the isolated zeros fall in lower- and mid-depth cells, which is compatible with a depth-related (technical) origin but does not prove one; cluster profiles are in zero_location.tsv (not summarised here); the excess z is defined only in 6 regions where NB expects at least one zero).
  8. Component instability: in the null replicates the median fraction of ZINB fits on the pi = 0 boundary is 1.00; median non-converged fraction NB 0.000, ZINB 0.000.

Question and why it matters

Method record E:3 allows hurdle, zero-inflated NB or latent count mixtures only after a simpler model misfits. Experiment 08 found NB explains the zeros of the deep chr7 windows but flagged some regions. Does a zero component add anything, and where? This decides whether a depth-aware evidence layer needs a zero-inflation term or whether quasi-Poisson/NB suffices. A supported zero component is a statistical description of the counts; it is not permission to delete zero cells.

Inputs

  • layers/raw, 3,460 cells; WNN clusters (5); exposure E_i = raw total minus target and flank counts (as in 08).
  • Regions (58): 7 saved windows, 21 further regions with nb_misfit_flag in 08 (called segments and random windows), 30 uncalled, unflagged random windows drawn with seed tuple (20261104, 9). The 08 table has 7 saved windows (not 6); all 7 are included. The negatives are windows with no called loss/gain in 08.
  • Held-out split: C.stratified_halves(lab, (20261104, 9)).

Method

y_i  target count of cell i;  mu_i = exp(log E_i + log n_t + x_i'beta),  x_i = intercept + cluster dummies
Poisson:   y ~ Pois(mu)
NB2:       y ~ NB(mu, alpha),  Var = mu + alpha mu^2,  p0_i = (1 + alpha mu_i)^(-1/alpha)
ZINB:      P(y=0) = pi_i + (1-pi_i) p0_i;  P(y=k) = (1-pi_i) NB(k),  logit pi_i = g0  [or g0 + g1 z_i, z = std. log E_i]
Hurdle NB: P(y=0) = pi0;  P(y=k) = (1-pi0) NB(k)/(1-p0_i) for k>0,  pi0 = (n0+0.5)/(n+1)
AIC = 2k - 2 ll;  BIC = k ln n - 2 ll
LRT:   LR = 2 (ll_ZINB - ll_NB) >= 0;  pi = 0 is on the boundary, so no chi-square reference.
       for b = 1..B (B=199): y* ~ NB(mu_hat, alpha_hat) (same E, clusters; seed (20261104, 9, region_index));
       refit NB and ZINB (2 starts) on y*; LR*_b. p = (1 + #{LR*_b >= LR}) / (B + 1)
pi interval: B_ci=99 replicates from the fitted ZINB (structural zeros with prob pi_i, seed (20261104, 9, region_index, 1)); refit ZINB; 2.5/97.5 percentiles
held-out: fit on one half, sum_i log p(y_i | fitted model) on the other half

Likelihoods are maximised with analytic gradients (L-BFGS-B; bounds on log alpha and logit pi). NB alpha is the ML estimate, so it differs slightly from 08's Cameron–Trivedi value.

Sensitivity

All-criteria support (p<0.05, BIC favours ZINB, held-out gain): 28/58 regions (15 negatives); nominal-only support (p<0.05 alone): 41/58. Three alternative zero parameterisations are reported: constant pi (used for the LRT), pi depending on log exposure (zinb_exposure, slope in lrt_by_region.tsv as pi_exposure_slope; LR in lr_obs_exposure, not bootstrapped), and the hurdle NB. The ranking by held-out score and BIC is in model_comparison.tsv. Results are conditional on the cluster terms and on the exposure definition from 08. The bootstrap resolution is 1/200; BH over 58 regions is a convenience and not a formal family-wise statement. NB alpha is the ML value here, not the Cameron–Trivedi value of 08.

Limits and alternative explanations

  • A rejected NB is also compatible with a misspecified mean (cluster terms too coarse, subclones) or heavier-tailed dispersion (e.g. alpha varying with exposure), not only with structural zeros.
  • The ZINB zero probability is not identifiable from a mean-model misfit; the held-out score is the check on whether the component predicts.
  • Regions were selected partly by experiment 08's NB misfit flag, so their p-values are conditional on selection; the 30 negatives are the unselected reference.
  • Random windows may contain real events. A zero component describes counts; it does not distinguish deletion, mappability, accessibility, or dropout.
  • Local optima are possible; 2 starts per ZINB fit and warm starts were used but the global optimum is not proven.

What the evidence supports

Supports

  • NB (not Poisson) is the necessary step for these counts; the zero component adds a handful of cells' worth of probability.
  • The deep chr7 saved windows are consistent with NB without extra zeros.
  • Isolated zero cells in cells with large expected counts are common in negative windows too (23/30), so a log-likelihood region score needs protection against them.
  • A parametric bootstrap with refitting is a workable non-regular test for this decision.

Does not support / cannot tell

  • Deleting or down-weighting zero cells.
  • Any statement that extra zeros are biological dropout, deletion or dosage.
  • A validated production zero-inflation model; this is an exploratory description.

Decision impact

For recommendation.md: the raw-count evidence layer can use NB (or quasi-Poisson) with exposure and cluster terms; a general zero-inflation component is not needed (pi is about 1 cell, and rejection is as common in negative windows as in flagged ones). The practical lesson is robustness: a single zero in a high-expected cell moves an NB log-likelihood by tens of units, so region scores should use count-level summaries or a capped per-cell contribution rather than a ZINB. Keep ZINB/hurdle only as an optional misfit diagnostic, reported with its bootstrap p and effect size, never as a cell filter.

Next checks

  • Re-run with region selection and assessment on disjoint cell halves.
  • Test alpha varying with exposure or cluster before attributing misfit to structural zeros.
  • Check flagged regions against a cross-sample technical baseline and mappability.
  • Increase B (e.g. 999) for the regions near p = 0.05.
