Bottom line

chr7 67.4–70.0 Mb is an even plateau at 0.13x its flanks (per-bin heterogeneity at percentile 0.3 of random windows), the other saved windows are uneven or shallow, and processed X attenuates the deepest low-GC dips (log2 attenuation up to 0.90) in line with the GC slope across random windows (-15.6 per unit GC difference).

Key findings

  1. Random 25-bin windows: SD of the log2 per-bin ratio is 0.51/0.80/1.27 (5th/50th/95th percentile); the 42 random windows with rr < 0.6 have median SD 0.69.
  2. chr7_67.4_70.0: SD 0.17, 100% of bins below 0.5x flank mean; thirds 0.14/0.12/0.12 — the flattest saved window (percentile 2 among deep random windows).
  3. chr7_70.0_73.3: SD 1.19 (percentile 93 of random windows); thirds 0.51/0.12/0.39 — a deep middle with shallower shoulders, not a plateau.
  4. chr7_8.3_10.7 and chr7_51.4_53.5 are fully depleted (all bins below 0.5x) with moderate heterogeneity (SD 0.62 and 0.49; percentiles 12 and 4 of random windows).
  5. chr9 windows are shallower and uneven: rr 0.73 (SD 1.07) and 0.50 (SD 0.96); only 44% and 64% of bins fall below 0.5x; 18 and 16 grid bins in them were removed by QC (experiment 02).
  6. Processed X attenuates the contrast: rr in X vs raw = 0.36 vs 0.20, 0.27 vs 0.16, 0.18 vs 0.13, 0.44 vs 0.34 for the four chr7 windows (log2 attenuation 0.39–0.90); their target GC is -0.046 to -0.009 relative to flanks.
  7. Across the 300 random windows, log2 attenuation regresses on target−flank GC with slope -15.6 and intercept 0.069; the regression-predicted attenuation for the chr7 windows is 0.21–0.79 against observed 0.39–0.90.

Question and why it matters

Question. Within each saved window, is the depth change an even plateau or patchy? Do processed values attenuate it relative to raw counts, and does that track GC? Where are QC-removed bins?

Why it matters. The recommendation reads the chr7 dips as most consistent with a shared regional baseline possibly combined with a smaller dosage change. An even plateau is what a dosage step would predict, while patchy depletion points to bin-level accessibility or mappability structure; the GC attenuation tests the claim that GC correction and regional biology are confounded at this scale. This is a descriptive contrast, not a test that separates the two.

Inputs

  • Depth. All-cell mean per-bin raw counts (layers/raw) and processed X from count_matrix.h5ad (3,460 cells × 25,337 bins); per-bin GC from var.GC.
  • Allele observations. Per-bin pooled AT from AT.parquet divided by raw counts, drawn in the figure (site-observation count, not unique fragments).
  • QC-removed grid positions. From pyepi_results/binned_genome.csv versus the retained grid (counts per window from experiment 02).
  • Regions. The 7 saved windows (6 windows + chr9 70–100 Mb comparison) with frozen 50-bin flanks on each side, and the 300 random 25-bin windows of experiment 04 (same rule and seed).
  • Not available. Per-fragment positions (no sub-100-kb resolution), mappability, and pre-QC counts for removed bins.

Method

  1. Express each target bin’s all-cell raw mean relative to the mean of the region’s flank bins (ratio per bin).
  2. Heterogeneity = SD of log2 per-bin ratios (ratios clipped at 1e-3 before log); also the share of bins below 0.5 and above 0.9, and the ratio in the first, middle and last third of the window.
  3. Place each saved window’s SD among the 300 random windows, and among the random windows with rr < 0.6 (matched depth).
  4. Processed-vs-raw attenuation: log2 of (rr in X / rr in raw). Regress it on the target−flank GC difference across the random windows and compare the saved windows with the regression line.
ratio_j        = raw_j / mean(raw over flank bins)             # per target bin j
sd_log2        = SD_j log2(max(ratio_j, 1e-3))
thirds         = mean(raw) over first / middle / last len//3 bins / mean(flank)
atten_log2     = log2( (mean X_target / mean X_flank) / (mean raw_target / mean raw_flank) )
atten ~ slope * (GC_target - GC_flank) + intercept      # least squares over random windows

Deterministic apart from the reused random-window rule (seed tuple (20261104, 4), numpy PCG64, shared with experiment 04). Percentiles are of the random-window distribution.

Sensitivity

Heterogeneity is reported against all random windows and against matched-depth random windows (rr < 0.6); the attenuation prediction uses a linear regression over 300 windows. Not varied: the third-based split, the log2 clipping at 1e-3, the 50-bin flank width and the 25-bin random width.

Limits and alternative explanations

  • Per-bin heterogeneity is a descriptive shape cue. Wide windows can contain a narrower event, so a patchy profile does not exclude a focal change, and an even plateau does not prove dosage.
  • Bins are 100 kb pooled over all cells: sub-bin structure and cell-level mixture are not visible here.
  • Processed X attenuates or exaggerates contrasts depending on the GC difference between target and flanks: per-cell GC LOESS rescales low-GC regions, so GC correction and regional biology are confounded at this scale; the regression shows association, not mechanism.
  • SD depends on the number of bins and on depth; saved windows (19–295 bins) are compared with 25-bin random windows.
  • QC-removed bins are positions, not measurements; their content is unknown.

What the evidence supports

Supports

  • chr7 67.4–70.0 Mb is an even plateau, consistent with a uniform change over the window (dosage or a regional baseline).
  • The other saved windows are less even; chr7_70.0_73.3 and the chr9 windows have wide bin-to-bin spread.
  • GC correction attenuates low-GC dips, with attenuation tracking the target−flank GC difference across random windows.

Does not support / cannot tell

  • That an even plateau is dosage rather than a regional accessibility or mappability baseline.
  • That patchy windows lack a focal change.
  • Any statement about the contents of QC-removed bins.

Decision impact

Supports the recommendation that raw-count contrast, not processed X, should carry the depth evidence, and that GC correction can hide real or baseline structure; the plateau at chr7 67.4–70.0 Mb makes a cross-sample baseline and mappability track the decisive missing experiment (rank 3).

Next checks

  • Compare the same windows in unrelated samples with a mappability track, to see whether the plateau recurs.
  • Repeat the heterogeneity comparison with width-matched random windows for each saved window.
  • Test whether the 19q13-qter raw excess lost by GC correction has the same shape in an independent sample.
