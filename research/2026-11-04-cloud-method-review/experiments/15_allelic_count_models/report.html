Experiment 15: observation models for allelic counts per cell

Which likelihood fits depth, the allele total AT and the haplotype split at the cell level? Exploratory diagnostic on MCF10-A development data; model comparison, not copy-number truth.

Bottom line

Per cell and region, the allele total AT is adequately described by a negative binomial (NB2) with an exposure offset and cluster terms: Poisson is clearly worse (-0.0242 [-0.0253, -0.0232] nats per cell-region vs NB2) and adding a zero-inflation or hurdle component changes the held-out score by +0.0004 (ZINB), i.e. by less than the 0.002 nats margin used here, although low-coverage cells show a small excess of zeros (about 1.4 points above NB2 expectation). The haplotype split HB | AT needs a beta-binomial (or equivalent variance-inflated binomial) conditional on AT, not a plain binomial (-0.0303 [-0.0315, -0.0291] nats per cell-region vs BB), and its cell-level rho is not one number: it falls from about 0.80 where cells carry ~1.5 observations to about 0.002 where they carry tens, while the variance inflation stays near 1.5.

Key findings

  1. Poisson is rejected for both count modalities. NB2 beats Poisson by 0.024 nats per cell-region for AT (95% CI -0.025 to -0.023 for Poisson minus NB2, 1731 held-out cells) and by 0.39 for raw depth (-0.46 to -0.34). ZIP is also worse than NB2 (-0.0148), so a zero term on a Poisson base does not help.
  2. Excess zero mass in AT is small. ZINB minus NB2 = +0.0004 [+0.0001, +0.0007] (raw-depth offset) and +0.0004 [+0.0001, +0.0008] (AT-outside offset); hurdle-NB -0.0003 [-0.0008, +0.0001]. ZINB is preferred in 62% of regions but by amounts below 0.002. In the lowest coverage tercile of called and random regions the observed zero fraction exceeds the NB2 prediction by 1.4 points on average (ZINB: -0.0).
  3. AT is more overdispersed than depth. Median NB2 alpha is 0.12 for AT (saved windows 0.57) versus 0.020 for depth; the dispersion parameter cannot be shared between modalities. Median 14% of cells have AT = 0 in a region; the saved chr7/chr9 windows have 68% zeros, which NB2 given depth predicts (69%).
  4. Offset choice: NB2 with log depth in the region as exposure scores better by +0.0490 [+0.0468, +0.0515] nats (raw-depth offset minus AT-outside offset), but the raw-depth offset puts the region's own fragments in the exposure and builds in the depth-AT dependence (C).
  5. HB | AT is overdispersed relative to a binomial but needs no mixture. Binomial(p) minus BB(p,rho): -0.0303 [-0.0315, -0.0291]; the orientation-unknown two-component mixture is -0.0014 [-0.0017, -0.0012]; a beta-binomial with constant variance inflation phi is -0.0016 [-0.0021, -0.0010] (all relative to a region-specific BB).
  6. Cell-level rho is far above the bin-level 0.0038 but the inflation is similar. Pooled family rho: saved 0.011, called 0.045, random 0.116, literature 0.0044 (1.1 to 30 times exp 06). Region rho falls with mean AT per cell (Spearman -0.97 on log-log); cell-level Pearson dispersion of HB | AT is 1.27 to 1.58 across AT strata, versus 1.20 to 1.53 for exp 06 bins. rho is therefore not transferable between units; the variance inflation is.
  7. HA and HB as separate counts predict almost as well as AT x conditional split. Joint log P(HA,HB) relative to NB(AT) x BB: independent NB -0.0009 [-0.0012, -0.0006], independent ZINB +0.0006 [+0.0001, +0.0011], ZINB(AT) x BB +0.0004 [+0.0001, +0.0007]. All within 0.002 nats.
  8. AT and depth are dependent. After cluster and exposure adjustment the Pearson-residual correlation is 0.29 (regions 0.11 to 0.57; permutation p < 0.01 in 100% of regions) and AT scales sub-proportionally with region depth (median elasticity 0.50, range 0.14 to 1.17). AT per raw fragment does not vary with coverage tercile (p<0.01 in 1% of regions) but does vary by cluster in 15%.

Question and why it matters

A combined depth + allele likelihood multiplies a depth term and an allele term. If the allele term uses a wrong count model (for example Poisson for AT, or a binomial split without overdispersion), the allele evidence is over-weighted and overdispersed noise is read as signal. Depth is already known to be NB2 with exposure (experiment 08). This experiment asks the same question of the allelic modality at the cell level, region by region: how should AT be modelled (many cells have AT = 0), how should the haplotype split HB | AT be modelled, can HA and HB simply be modelled as two counts, and how dependent are AT and depth? The aim is to pick an observation model per modality by held-out evidence, not by convention.

Inputs

  • AnnData count_matrix.h5ad (layers/raw; 3460 cells) and WNN cluster labels (5 clusters).
  • HA, HB, AT parquet (bins x barcodes, joined by barcode name; coordinates equal to AnnData; AT == HA + HB in all regions: True; integer valued). Column order differs from AnnData, set equality holds; matrices were re-ordered by name.
  • Regions (138): 7 saved windows, 4 literature loci, 67 called short segments (experiment 04 rule), and 60 random 25-bin windows drawn without replacement from 12,890 eligible windows (no gap, every bin cn_call == 1 in the `all` karyogram), seed tuple (20261104, 15).
  • Train/test: stratified_halves(labels, (20261104, 15)), 1729 train, 1731 test cells; the reverse direction is a sensitivity. Experiment 06 pooled bin-level rho = 0.0038 and experiment 08 depth results are read for comparison.

Method

A. count y_ir (cell i, region r), cluster dummies g_c
   log mu_ir = offset_ir + b0 + g_c(i)                       offset coefficient fixed at 1
   Poisson : P(y) = mu^y e^-mu / y!
   NB2     : var = mu + alpha mu^2                           alpha fitted by ML
   ZIP/ZINB: P(0) = pi + (1-pi) f(0), P(y>0) = (1-pi) f(y)   pi = expit(g0 + g1 z), z = standardised offset
   hurdle  : P(0) = pi,  P(y>0) = (1-pi) f_NB(y) / (1 - f_NB(0))
   offsets : raw_in = log(depth_in_region + 0.5)             [AT only]
             out    = log(AT outside region + 0.5)  (AT);  log(raw outside region + 0.5)  (depth)
   score   : mean over held-out cells of log P(y_ir | train fit); difference to NB2 averaged over regions of a family,
             bootstrap over test cells (1000 resamples, seed (20261104,15,1)).
B. HB | AT = n, cells with n >= 1
   Bin(0.5); Bin(p); BB(0.5,rho); BB(p,rho); BB(p, rho_n = (phi-1)/(n-1)) (constant variance inflation);
   w Bin(p) + (1-w) Bin(1-p) with p >= 0.5.   BB variance = n p (1-p) [1 + (n-1) rho]
   pooled rho per family: grid MLE of one rho shared by regions (p per region fixed), bootstrap over cells (400).
   joint log P(HA,HB): NB(HA) NB(HB) | ZINB(HA) ZINB(HB) | NB(AT) Bin(HB|AT) | NB(AT) BB | ZINB(AT) BB | ZINB(AT) mixture   (offset log(raw_in + 0.5))
C. e_AT, e_depth = Pearson residuals of NB2 fits (cluster terms, outside exposures); corr(e_AT, e_depth), permutation within cluster x tercile;
   log E[AT] = log AT_out + g_c + b (log(depth_in + 0.5) - mean);  AT/depth by tercile and cluster (quasi-Poisson Wald F).
All optimisation: L-BFGS-B, numerical gradients; Poisson IRLS start. No scientific code from the pipeline is changed.

Limits and alternative explanations

  • These are observation-model fits per region; they say nothing about copy number. A model that predicts AT better is not evidence about dosage.
  • Held-out scores differ by a few thousandths of a nat for zero-inflation. The margin of 0.002 is a judgement; with 1,730 test cells a difference of 0.0004 is statistically resolvable but not practically important.
  • Bootstrap over cells treats regions as fixed and cells as exchangeable within the half; cells in the same cluster share structure, and the two halves share the same donors/library, so intervals are optimistic for transfer to a new sample.
  • AT counts selected-site depth, not independent fragments. Reads from one haplotype molecule at several SNPs, or duplicate fragments, create within-cell correlation (high rho at small AT) that has nothing to do with biology.
  • The raw-depth offset includes the region's own fragments. That is useful for prediction but makes AT and depth mechanically dependent; the AT-outside offset avoids it but ignores region-specific accessibility.
  • Region choice: saved and literature families have 7 and 4 regions only; called segments were selected because the pipeline called them; random windows are restricted to bins with cn_call == 1 in the `all` karyogram and are not neutral truth.
  • ZINB and hurdle fits use a constant-plus-offset zero model. A zero process tied to the number of heterozygous sites in reach (not observable per cell here) could be misrepresented, so 'no excess zeros' means no excess that this simple zero model can find.
  • Phase labels HA/HB come from the pipeline's SNP phasing; mislabelled phase would inflate rho. The orientation-unknown mixture did not outperform, but phasing quality is not assessed here.
  • Only one dataset (MCF10-A development data) and one 100 kb bin grid; regions are 25 bins or more.

What the evidence supports

Supports

  • NB2 with exposure is an adequate count likelihood for AT; Poisson, ZIP are not.
  • Zeros in AT are mostly explained by depth and overdispersion; the leftover excess is a small low-coverage effect.
  • A conditional beta-binomial with variance-inflation dispersion is adequate for HB | AT; a plain binomial and a fixed rho carried over from the bin level are not.
  • AT and depth are positively dependent per cell beyond exposure, so a combined model cannot treat them as independent given copy number.

Does not support / cannot tell

  • Anything about copy number or allelic imbalance at any locus; these are observation-model comparisons.
  • That no structural-zero process exists: a zero model linked to heterozygous-site density per cell could not be fitted from these data.
  • That rho = 0.0038 is wrong at the bin level; only that it does not transfer to cell level (the variance inflation does).
  • Independence of the halves: both are from the same cells' library, so generalisation to another sample is untested.

Decision impact

Supports recommendation.md item 4 (a joint depth-allele likelihood evaluated later) only with specific components: NB2 for depth, NB2 with its own alpha for AT, and a beta-binomial split with constant-phi dispersion, plus an explicit AT-depth dependence term (shared cell effect or AT conditioned on depth) because residual correlation is 0.29. It argues against reusing the bin-level rho = 0.0038 at cell level and against Poisson/binomial shortcuts. It does not change the rank-1 evidence layer.

Next checks

  • Score the constant-phi beta-binomial against a phi that depends on SNP count per bin (needs the per-site table, not available here).
  • Fit a joint depth-AT-split likelihood with a shared cell random effect and check whether the residual AT-depth correlation falls to zero.
  • Repeat on an independent sample and with a held-out donor/library to test transfer of alpha and phi.
  • Obtain heterozygous-site counts per cell and region to test whether the small low-coverage zero excess is 'no informative SNP in reach'.
  • Add a phasing-quality check (random re-labelling of phase) to separate technical from biological split overdispersion.
