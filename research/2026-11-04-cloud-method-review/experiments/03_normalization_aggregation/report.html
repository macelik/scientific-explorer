18 of 18 source × estimand fits end at the lower edge of the s grid (s = 0.5 × median segment mean). There the fitted scale is not estimated from the data: the median-weighted segment is defined as k = 2, and every call becomes a fixed threshold on segment mean / genome median (Holmes loss below about 0.68, Watson loss at or below 0.5, Holmes gain above about 1.22, Watson gain at or above 1.5). The reference level is the genome median of the same profile, which broad events and baseline structure can move.
The weights (2, 1.5, 0.5, 0.33, 0.25, 0.2, 0.17 for k = 0…6) make the loss/base cut-point fall at m/s ≈ 1.37 instead of 1.5 and the base/gain cut-point at ≈ 2.45 instead of 2.5. Losses therefore need a deeper contrast than gains, and the Watson rule (≤ 1 loss, ≥ 3 gain) is stricter still; the combined call takes 0.5/1.5 when only Holmes moves. The code comment says the weights penalize deviations from diploid, but weights fall monotonically above k = 2, so higher states are cheaper than k = 2.

Interpretation

  • Calls depend on the estimand: processed-X means, exposure-weighted raw rates and equal-weight raw rates assign different states on the same boundaries (A). The raw estimands have no GC correction, so this mixes GC with aggregation effects; read it as a sensitivity range, not as one estimand being right.
  • The relative CN scale is fitted genome-wide, so a call in one region can change when another chromosome is added or removed (C). A per-region evidence report should state the reference level it was compared against.
