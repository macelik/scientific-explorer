Bottom line

Boundaries are reproducible between disjoint halves of the cells (split-half r = 0.996–0.999, cell sampling ≤ 0.18% of pseudobulk variance) and the select-then-test scan accepted 1 of 300 exchangeable nulls at α = 0.001, so a significant boundary shows reproducible non-sampling structure, not that the structure is copy number.

Key findings

  1. Exchangeable null: 0/150 (120 bins) and 1/150 (400 bins) first splits accepted at α = 0.001; pooled 1/300 (upper 95% Clopper–Pearson bound 1.8%, i.e. not excluding rates above α).
  2. Fixed-split p-values ignore the search over cuts: median p_local 0.22 (120 bins) and 0.058 (400 bins) versus median max-over-cuts p 0.45 and 0.74 (20 trials each, 200 permutations).
  3. Cell sampling explains 0.06% of the all pseudobulk bin variance (robust SD 0.048 of half-difference vs 2.049 full) and 0.18% for cluster 0; split-half correlation 0.9989 / 0.9963.
  4. Harness: the rerun reproduces the saved genome-pool breakpoints exactly for 4 of 4 source/chromosome scans (all/chr7 15; all/chr9 10; cluster0/chr7 12; cluster0/chr9 8 breakpoints).
  5. Half-split stability (±2 bins in both halves): all/chr7 11/15, all/chr9 5/10, cluster0/chr7 12/12, cluster0/chr9 8/8 (36 of 45 overall).
  6. 10 of 14 saved-window edges lie within 200 kb of a rerun breakpoint, and 10 of those 10 reappear in both halves within ±2 bins; windows with no edge breakpoint: chr9_20_27, chr9_70_100.
  7. A sampling-only profile (half-difference) still yields accepted breakpoints in all (3 on chr7 at 51.1, 77.4, 146.7 Mb, 2 on chr9) and none in cluster 0, with segment SD tracking depth (e.g. chr7 51.1–77.4 Mb SD 0.032 at pseudobulk mean 2.11): AD responds to variance changes as well as means.

Question and why it matters

Question. Do the pinned local/global permutation tests, as used after choosing the maximum-AD cut, control false acceptance? How much of the cluster pseudobulk’s bin-to-bin variation is cell-sampling noise, and are boundaries reproducible between disjoint halves of the same cells?

Why it matters. The recommendation states that the saved boundaries are reproducible and that the problem is interpreting them. The review started from the hypothesis that the select-then-test procedure is anti-conservative; this experiment tests that hypothesis and quantifies how much boundary structure is cell sampling versus reproducible non-sampling structure, which determines whether a significant boundary can be used as evidence of copy-number change in the per-region evidence layer.

Inputs

  • Matrix. processed X of count_matrix.h5ad (3,460 cells × 25,337 bins), averaged per group: all (3,460 cells) and WNN cluster0 (1,286 cells), each also split into halves A and B (pseudobulks.npz).
  • Chromosomes scanned. chr7 and chr9 (the chromosomes of the saved windows). The null uses the all-cell chr7 profile.
  • Saved breakpoints. genome_pool/raw_accepted_breakpoints.tsv (harness check) and the workbench pool of the all-cell pseudobulk for the global permutation.
  • Windows. The 7 saved target windows (shared regions.tsv, 1-based start coordinates).
  • Settings (workbench DEFAULTS). k = 6, min_child_bins = 20, min_terminal_bins = 50, 1,000 permutations, α = 0.001.
  • Not available. Per-cell-level segmentation of the production run (pyepi_results/ used k = 2 per cell, a different procedure), and any independent CN truth.

Method

  1. A. Selection-aware null. Randomly permute the bins of the all-cell chr7 pseudobulk (so no spatial structure remains), take the first L bins (L = 120 and 400; 150 trials each), pick the geometry-valid cut with maximal AD distance, then apply the pinned permutation_test_ad at that split (local) and global_permutation_test_ad against a permuted genome pool (global). Accept if both p < α. For the first 20 trials per L a max-over-cuts permutation p-value (200 permutations) is computed as the selection-aware comparator.
  2. B. Sampling share. Split cells into two halves within each cluster; the half-difference D = (A − B)/2 contains cell-sampling variation only. Compare robust SD of D with that of the full pseudobulk.
  3. C. Boundary stability. Scan chr7 and chr9 with server.allelic_scan.scan_chromosome (workbench DEFAULTS, genome pool) on the full profile, each half and the half-difference, for all and cluster0. A full-scan breakpoint is ‘stable’ if each half has a breakpoint within ±2 (or ±5) bins.
D = (A - B) / 2                       # pure cell-sampling variation
sampling_share = (MAD_sd(D) / MAD_sd(full))^2,  MAD_sd(v) = 1.4826 * median(|v - median(v)|)
split_half_corr = corr(A, B)
cut = argmax over geometry-valid cuts of AD distance;  accept iff p_local < 0.001 and p_global < 0.001
p_max = (1 + #{perms with max valid-cut AD >= observed}) / 201

Seeds: halves default_rng((20261104, 2)); null trials default_rng([20261104, 1, L, trial]) (numpy PCG64). Pinned statistics come from pyEpi e8305ea via server/pyepi_adapter. Nothing is written outside this folder.

Sensitivity

Two sequence lengths (120 and 400 bins) for the null; two sources (all, cluster0) and two stability tolerances (±2 and ±5 bins) for stability. Not varied: α, k, min_child_bins, number of permutations, the single halving seed, and the number of null trials (150 per L; the null acceptance rate is imprecise at this size).

Limits and alternative explanations

  • The exchangeable null removes spatial structure; it shows the permutation tests are not grossly loose on exchangeable data, not that significance of a boundary in structured data means CN. Reproducible accessibility, mappability and GC residue structure also rejects an exchangeable null.
  • Stability is assessed on halves of the same cells and the same sample: it shows boundaries are not cell-sampling artefacts but not that they are shared across samples or are CN.
  • 150 trials per length cannot distinguish an acceptance rate of 0.1% from several times that (see Clopper–Pearson bound).
  • Only chr7 and chr9 were scanned; all half-split results are for those chromosomes.
  • The production per-cell run used a different segmentation (k = 2 per cell), so these results describe the workbench cluster scan only.

What the evidence supports

Supports

  • Boundaries are not cell-sampling artefacts: halves of the same cells agree almost perfectly and most breakpoints reappear in both halves.
  • On exchangeable nulls the select-then-test AD scan rarely accepts at the stated α, so the p-values are not grossly anti-conservative in that setting.
  • Window edges that coincide with a breakpoint are reproduced in both halves.

Does not support / cannot tell

  • That a significant boundary indicates a copy-number change: reproducible accessibility, mappability and GC structure also reject an exchangeable null.
  • Calibration under spatially dependent nulls or in other samples.
  • Edge support for windows with no nearby breakpoint.

Decision impact

Supports the recommendation that significance of a segmentation boundary should not be used as CN evidence on its own, and that the per-region evidence layer must weigh depth contrast, coverage invariance and allelic evidence instead. It also shows the scan is variance-sensitive in low-depth regions, which argues for evaluating an exposure-aware raw-count segmentation (rank 4) only after a cross-sample baseline exists.

Next checks

  • Calibrate against nulls with realistic spatial dependence, e.g. block-permuted or baseline-structured profiles from unrelated samples.
  • Increase null trials to resolve the acceptance rate relative to α.
  • Repeat the half-split analysis on an independent sample.
