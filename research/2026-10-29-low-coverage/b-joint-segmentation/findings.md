# Joint segmentation and uncertainty

## Decision

First investigate whether the published **Numbat-multiome ATAC-bin** workflow can be supplied valid inputs; in parallel retain an exploratory **raw-count depth-only changepoint** comparator that can report unresolved allele status. A covariance-aware joint HMM is a longer-term research design, not an established drop-in solution. None can turn the current sample's identity into region-level truth. Three designs below are ranked by relevance and feasibility, not a claim of validation.

| Screen | Original evidence | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| Numbat-multiome ATAC-bin | Published scATAC, compared with WGS in tumor cohorts | Highest-priority published comparator; reference and phased SNP inputs are gaps. |
| Count/heteroscedastic change points | BIC-seq2 evaluated DNA sequencing; PELT is a general optimization method | Strong relative-depth comparator; transfer to accessibility and chosen likelihood untested. |
| Joint depth/allele HMM, Bayesian changepoints | Original Numbat is scRNA; Bayesian run-length inference is general sequential modeling | Candidate design only; dependence and phase block model must be justified. |
| FACETS / ASCAT | Paired bulk DNA sequencing / SNP arrays | Useful segmentation and identifiability ideas; not direct substitute for unmatched scATAC. |
| Selective prediction | General statistical learning | Output and evaluation framework, not a guarantee under sample shift. |
| Masked image denoising | Images and expression | Reject as CN evidence generator; predictive smoothing is not a new observation. |

## Candidate 1: published ATAC-bin comparator

[Numbat-multiome](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12531993/) explicitly supports scATAC separately and reports comparisons with WGS in selected tumor cohorts. Its [official input vignette](https://kharchenkolab.github.io/numbat/articles/numbat-multiome.html) describes raw fragment counts by bin, phased SNP-level allele counts, genomic bins and normalized ATAC reference profiles. Its paper constructs references from annotated normal cells; those assumptions cannot be imported into MCF10-A without evidence. This is direct assay evidence, not proof of performance in an unmatched homogeneous sample. Current exported HA/HB/AT bin matrices lack phase-set continuity; they do not by themselves establish the SNP-level phased input required. Exact missing-emission, genomic gap, and within-assay dependence handling require source-level audit before use. The published joint HMM and haplotype ideas are explained in [original Numbat methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10289836/). In its event-level arm/focal evaluation, segments below 10% of an arm or below 200 kb were excluded; this benchmark therefore does not establish performance for small focal events. Its separate bin-size analysis visually compared breakpoint granularity in one chromothripsis sample, not a general small-event accuracy benchmark ([methods and results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12531993/)). The current repository DESCRIPTION reports version 1.5.2 and MIT + file LICENSE; this is a moving branch, not a pinned tested version ([DESCRIPTION](https://github.com/kharchenkolab/numbat/blob/main/DESCRIPTION), [license metadata](https://github.com/kharchenkolab/numbat/blob/main/LICENSE)). No local runtime was measured.

## Candidate 2: depth-first count segmentation, conditional allele assessment

Model observed raw depth counts with per-cell exposure and region-specific accessibility/technical covariates using a checked Poisson or overdispersed likelihood, with a genomic-distance-aware boundary penalty and explicit callable masks. A zero is a likelihood observation; a missing or filtered bin has no emission. Carry coordinates and missing span into a boundary interval rather than pretending adjacent retained bins were physically adjacent. A depth change remains possible when allele data are absent. This is relative accessibility-normalized depth, not absolute CN. Compare candidate count models using predictive checks, and represent uncertainty and alternative baselines; a shared dip may be shared technical bias. BIC-seq2 illustrates normalization and segmentation for DNA read counts, not validated ATAC corrections ([paper, methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5772337/)). PELT optimizes a penalized objective with linear expected complexity only under its conditions; arbitrary covariate-dependent costs do not automatically satisfy pruning assumptions ([paper](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.2012.737745)).

Assess alleles conditional on a segment only when ascertainment, phase blocks and counting units are understood. Segment discovery and assessment on the same counts creates selection bias; pre-specified boundaries or held-out evidence and selection-aware evaluation are needed. Do not multiply naive independent depth and AT likelihoods: the local builder sums SNP-site DP, potentially with repeated molecules, and depth and AT are coupled ([shared brief](../shared/WAVE1_BRIEF.md)). Conditional factorization is valid only after a generative measurement model is justified. No normal is needed to locate a relative shift, but no reference-free relative change uniquely identifies a deletion, diploid baseline or absolute ploidy.

## Candidate 3: joint model with missingness and dependence

A future HMM or Bayesian segmentation could model raw depth and allele observations jointly with shared latent exposure or a validated covariance/fragment-level observation model. At each bin marginalize an unavailable allele observation, preserving a depth emission; an observed zero is different. Model phase-set orientation as block-specific latent variables or analyze blocks separately; never globally pool HA/HB without demonstrable orientation. Treat SNP ascertainment and missingness as potentially informative, not automatically missing at random. Use actual genomic distances in transition priors and report posterior boundary mass across gaps, including where no unique boundary is identifiable. Bayesian run-length probabilities depend on their emission and hazard assumptions, and a posterior is not automatically calibrated ([Adams and MacKay](https://arxiv.org/abs/0710.3742)). Fit, runtime and calibration remain unknown.

[FACETS](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5027494/) jointly segments depth and allelic log odds and uses matched normal data to reduce allele mapping bias; its heterozygote weighting does not invent missing sites, and its later parametric model makes independence assumptions that should not be transferred automatically. [ASCAT](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2947907/) illustrates that ploidy and admixture create multiple solutions even in DNA data. Without a defensible anchor, allow several relative-state explanations rather than assigning integer or homozygous-deletion states.

## Output semantics and evaluation

For every interval report callable span and gap, raw count/exposure and uncertainty, boundary support or range, baseline dependence, phase information and number of informative sites. Separately label: **allele unobserved**; **insufficient evidence** for a prespecified imbalance; **compatible with balance** (not proof); **evidence against a specified imbalance** only if a prespecified uncertainty interval or power criterion supports it; or **evidence consistent with imbalance** under stated model assumptions. Report depth-only boundaries as such. No allele observations must never become 50:50 pseudo-counts. Establish risk versus fraction of intervals receiving a call on held-out independent truth datasets; selective prediction trades coverage for risk but does not provide an automatic cross-dataset guarantee ([selective classification paper](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2017/hash/4a8423d5e91fda00bb7e46540e2b0cf1-Abstract.html)).

[Noise2Self](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.11365) uses noise independence assumptions to predict masked values; genomic accessibility correlations, coupled depth and SNP counts, and irregular gaps make this an untested transfer. Imputed values cannot be counted as independent allele evidence and smoothing can obscure focal boundaries.

## Recommended next experiment

First recover and audit SNP-level observations, phase-set boundaries, fragment/count units and reference provenance; define whether a defensible independent reference exists. Then use frozen candidate boundaries and observed missingness, comparing a depth-only count model with an explicitly derived joint model under simulated measurement scenarios reflecting that audit. Pre-register alternative effect sizes, phase switches, dependence strengths, gap patterns and seed 20261029 with child streams; simulations test assumptions, not biological truth. **Decision criterion:** retain a joint candidate only if it preserves depth-only boundary detection when alleles are missing, does not inflate false positives relative to depth-only under coupled null simulations, and demonstrates calibrated uncertainty and a useful risk–coverage curve on held-out independently characterized samples. Set quantitative tolerances before running; without independent samples or an identified observation model, report feasibility only and abstain from claims of biological validation. MCF10-A and the 300-cell panel are development data, not independent truth or a generalization benchmark.
