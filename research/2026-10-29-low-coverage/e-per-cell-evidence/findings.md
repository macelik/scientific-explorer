# Per-cell loss evidence: first-pass review

## Decision

Start with raw regional count models and conditional allele-power calculations, reporting relative depletion and uncertainty rather than assigning absolute copy states. A zero or low count is compatible with sampling, low accessibility, mapping/filtering or loss; neither a zero-inflated component nor a fitted low-count cluster identifies deletion. The present MCF10-A sample is a non-malignant cell line, not a labelled normal or a mixture of tumour and normal cells. Region-specific power is **not yet quantified**. The subsequent descriptive pilot below reviewed regional counts; phase consistency, overdispersion and independent CN truth remain unestablished.

**Coordinator update after cross-task review (2026-10-29):** the original Numbat paper discussed below is an RNA study, but Numbat-multiome now has published scATAC evaluation. Its joint model includes beta-binomial allelic emissions, so this model family is not wholly untested in scATAC. That does not validate a proposed regional test on these sparse, aggregated observations. See [the 2025 paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12531993/) and [task A's updated review](../a-cnv-methods/findings.md).

## Screen and ranking

| Candidate | Decision and evidence |
|---|---|
| Poisson and negative-binomial (NB) exposure regression | First diagnostic; transferred count-model idea, not validated scATAC CN caller. Poisson supplies a sampling benchmark; NB allows variance above the mean. ScRNA studies show both that ordinary counts can explain many zeros and that the conclusion depends on data and baseline model ([Svensson 2020](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41587-019-0379-5); [Choi et al. 2020](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13059-020-02103-2)). Neither result establishes an scATAC distribution. |
| Phase-aware beta-binomial allele model | Second diagnostic, especially useful for power and abstention. The original Numbat study models per-SNP allele counts with beta-binomial overdispersion and phase switches in scRNA ([Gao et al., Methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10289836/)); the newer scATAC extension is noted above. Its individual-cell aggregation uses a binomial approximation after phasing; do not assume that approximation holds here. A new test on this dataset remains exploratory. |
| Hurdle, zero-inflated or latent mixtures | Exploratory sensitivity comparison only if the simpler baseline predictively fails. Extra zeros depend on the assumed null and latent class membership is not a copy-state label. Mixture testing is nonregular, so an ordinary chi-square likelihood-ratio p-value is not generally justified ([Chen and Kalbfleisch](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0378375804001569)). |
| Alleloscope | Assay-relevant context, not an extra per-cell count model recommendation: it studies allele-specific CNAs in scATAC and requires segmentation; the paper describes several segmentation inputs and assumptions, including use of matched DNA when available ([Wu et al., segmentation methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9127226/)). Its full caller is outside this bounded model review. |

## Proposed count comparison

For cell i and region r, model raw integer count with a log library exposure, region-specific baseline, coverage and cluster covariates and NB dispersion; compare to Poisson and matched regions with similar callable span, mappability, accessibility and GC where available. Exposure should be estimated excluding the focal region and checked for composition bias. Keep processed X as a separate descriptive output, never as count input. Compare observed and posterior-predictive zero fractions, quantiles and positive-count distributions within coverage strata and matched regions; use held-out cells/regions where possible, preserving cell and genomic correlation. Report effect intervals and cell-level evidence, not hard labels from low counts. WNN groups are coverage-confounded: report with and without cluster adjustment, and do not interpret groups as independent clones.

A candidate focal loss can be diluted by a wider segment: inspect the fixed unmerged candidate and subregion/bin pattern against flanks, accounting for gaps and correlated bins. Since regions were selected from observed depth, naïve confidence intervals and p-values are conditional on selection; use an independent held-out selection/assessment split or rerun selection within a calibration scheme, and predefine the family across all regions and models.

## Allelic information and identifiability

For each cell and region, retain HA, HB and AT separately, audit phase sets and SNP ascertainment, and model haplotype counts conditional on AT with a beta-binomial where estimable. Include bias and contamination sensitivity and possible phase switches; do not sum haplotypes over incompatible phase blocks. Opposite allelic states can cancel in a pseudobulk and pooling helps only when an effect is shared ([Numbat, Results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10289836/)). A count of AT=0 supplies no allelic fraction; a low AT with a wide interval is unresolved, not evidence of balance or absence of LOH. Before any negative claim, choose a biologically meaningful alternative fraction, estimate the detection probability under observed AT and plausible bias/dispersion, and report the minimum detectable effect or interval; account for multiple testing and selection. No numerical power is asserted without these inputs.

| Hypothesis | Expected observable pattern under simplified assumptions | Why it remains ambiguous / needed evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Technical or accessibility depletion | Reduced raw counts and often SNP observations, possibly consistently across groups | Can resemble shared loss; compare matched controls and mapping/GC/accessibility priors; AT and raw depth may count overlapping molecules and are not independent. |
| Hemizygous loss | Relative depth depletion and coherent haplotype imbalance if heterozygous sites are observed and phase is reliable | Unknown baseline, mixtures, mapping bias, contamination and accessibility can mimic or attenuate either signal; need orthogonal DNA and phase validation. |
| Biallelic loss | Very low DNA dosage and sparse or absent allele observations in an ideal uncontaminated sample | AT=0 cannot distinguish no copies from no usable SNP observations; even nonzero raw counts can result from contamination or mismapping. Requires independent locus-level DNA measurement plus a calibrated background. |

Relative state and absolute ploidy remain unidentified without an anchor; allele imbalance can also arise from copy-neutral LOH or allele-specific accessibility. No independence assumption between AT and depth is justified until count provenance is known. Fitting a joint product likelihood without modeling their shared reads can overstate certainty.

## Calibration and next experiment

Freeze regions and controls and audit observation units and phase-set boundaries first. Then report each region's raw and processed depth separately, per-cell AT distribution, count-zero rates by coverage strata, eligible SNP/phase blocks and callable span. Predefine a plausible allele-fraction alternative and bias/dispersion grid, and calculate conditional detection power or interval width using the actual AT distribution. **Decision criterion:** advance an allelic test only if prespecified coverage, phasing and sensitivity analyses show it can reliably distinguish the chosen alternative at the chosen error rate; otherwise label that region unresolved. Numerical power targets and alternatives require an explicit decision before running the experiment.

If a mixture model is later justified, simulate from fitted nulls with the same exposure and dependence structure, repeat fitting and selection within each replicate, assess false-positive rate and confidence coverage, and use held-out data and orthogonal DNA truth. A parametric bootstrap is conditional on the adequacy of the null and is not a universal guarantee. The 300-cell panel is a feasibility sample, not independent validation; simulation reflects its assumptions rather than biological ground truth.

## Shared-panel descriptive pilot

In the fixed 300 cells, raw zero **bins** were common (51.1–81.5% across the six candidate windows), whereas entire raw regions were zero in 0–22 cells. The largest whole-region zero count was chr7 51.4–53.5 Mb (22/300); chr7 67.4–70.0 Mb had 10/300 and chr7 8.3–10.7 Mb had 7/300. The other three windows had none. These counts make a zero-heavy sampling distribution descriptively plausible, but no expected-zero baseline or mixture fit was computed: neither excess structural zeros nor a deletion component has been established. Coverage and cluster stratifications are in [the table](artifacts/zero_support_by_stratum.tsv); their combination is uneven and these descriptive groupings are not independent biological replicates.

AT was zero across the entire window in 116–255 cells depending on region; per-window median AT was 0 or 1 and no cell had AT sum ≥10. AT is a sum of SNP-site observations and may count a molecule repeatedly, so these thresholds are descriptive only, not independent-trial counts or an allelic power calculation. Phase continuity is unverified. No HA/HB interpretation was attempted.

Raw target counts per retained megabase were lower than both flanks in every window: target/left 0.14–0.63 and target/right 0.12–0.91. Processed ratios were 0.18–0.84 and 0.19–0.78, respectively. These are ratios of cohort mean densities, not matched-cell effects; processed and raw values have different meanings. Flanks use 50 retained bins and may overlap candidate events, and unequal gaps/accessibility affect comparisons. In particular, chr9 20–27 Mb has raw target/right 0.91 while the narrower 22.5–26.6 Mb window has 0.56, consistent with possible dilution but also with local heterogeneity; this is not evidence identifying an event. See [density table](artifacts/flank_density_comparison.tsv) and [pilot](pilot.md).
